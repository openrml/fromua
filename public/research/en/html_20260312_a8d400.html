"Career Change Strategist (Wartime Version): When Your Old Job No Longer Pays or Inspires"

Date: March 11, 2026 | Researcher: LLM Role Architect | Status: Final Report

1. Research Objective

To create a scientifically grounded, balanced, and ethical OpenRML role "Career Change Strategist (Wartime Version)" that helps people whose professions have become obsolete due to war, who have lost their jobs, businesses, or are forced to retrain (veterans, IDPs, people with disabilities, lost professions). The role must address the needs of Ukrainians in the context of war and be based on international evidence-based practices (career coaching, adaptation psychology, microgrants, digital platforms).

3. Terminology: Key Concepts

Economic Adaptation: The process of transitioning military personnel to civilian life, including employment, retraining, development of entrepreneurial skills, and use of creative potential. Research shows that 58% of servicemen are only partially ready for reintegration .

Career Development Interventions (CDIs): Structured programs (career counseling, mentoring, coaching) that help adults navigate complex labor markets. Research shows varying effectiveness, with mentoring and specialized interventions improving job satisfaction and self-efficacy .

"Translation" of Military Experience: The process of transforming skills acquired during service into the "language" of the civilian labor market. The Veteran Career platform helps create resumes based on military experience and identify where these skills are most in demand .

Self-Employment and Entrepreneurship: 26% of veterans intend to start their own business, which could become a driver of economic growth . State support programs include grants up to 15,000 UAH for retraining , interest-free loans up to 430,000 UAH for IDPs , and salary compensation for employers hiring veterans .

Psychological Support and Resilience: A study among Ukrainian educators showed that available psychological support reduces career change intentions by 6%, and self-help practices by 12% . This highlights the importance of integrating psychological support into career counseling.

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1: Economic Adaptation of Servicemen in Ukraine (Uran, 2025)

Title: Research of problems and prospects of economic adaptation of military servicemen: sociological and statistical analysis
Link: Uran
Key Findings:
  • 58% of servicemen are only partially ready for reintegration into civilian life .
  • Main needs: access to educational programs (48%), entrepreneurship support (34%), development of digital (22%) and creative (28%) skills .
  • High potential for self-employment: 26% intend to start their own business, which could become a driver of economic growth .
  • Need for an integrated institutional support model combining state policy, digital tools, human capital development, and use of creative potential .

📌 Source 2 & 10: Ukrainian State Initiatives (Cabinet of Ministers, 2025-2026)

Title: Veteran Career platform / Government adopts comprehensive measures to develop the labour market
Link: Cabinet of Ministers 2026 | Cabinet of Ministers 2025
Key Findings:
  • Veteran Career platform: over 65,000 jobs, help "translating" military experience into civilian skills, resume creation .
  • Veteran support: increased salary up to 16,000 UAH for public works, salary compensation for employers (100% for 6 months), assistance with grants and training .
  • "Obriy" digital ecosystem: unemployment registration through Diia, grants up to 15,000 UAH for retraining, AI job matching .

📌 Source 3: IDP Pathway - Digital Tool for IDPs (Cabinet of Ministers, 2025)

Title: Ministry of Social Policy, Family and Unity develops IDP Pathway digital tool
Link: Cabinet of Ministers
Key Findings:
  • Development of the IDP Pathway digital tool to support IDPs from evacuation to integration .
  • Priorities: housing, systemic support, reintegration, and job search .
  • Importance of recognizing the loss of not only housing but also social connections and sense of future .

📌 Source 4: Scoping Review of Career Development Interventions for Adults (Macquarie University, 2025)

Title: Unlocking career potential: a scoping review of recent interventions for adult career development
Link: Macquarie University
Key Findings:
  • Career Development Interventions (CDIs) are important for adults navigating complex labor markets .
  • Review of 20 studies identified four types of interventions: career counseling, mentoring, career coaching, and specialized programs .
  • Results show variable effectiveness: mentoring and specialized interventions improve job satisfaction and self-efficacy .
  • Emphasis on the need for standardized designs and consistent outcome measures .

📌 Source 5: Institutional Mental Health Support and Career Stability (Pollux, 2025)

Title: Time to act: Institutional mental health support for Ukrainian academic staff during wartime
Link: Pollux
Key Findings:
  • Study of 429 Ukrainian university educators showed that almost half frequently think about changing careers .
  • Available psychological support is associated with a 6% reduction in career change intentions .
  • Self-help proved most effective, reducing career change intentions by 12% .
  • Highlights the importance of structured psychological support for retaining personnel .

📌 Source 6: Reboot Program for Veterans (Red Cross, 2025)

Title: An information session for veterans and their families was held in the Lviv region as part of the Ukrainian Red Cross's Reboot programme
Link: Red Cross
Key Findings:
  • The "Reboot" program helps vulnerable groups (veterans, IDPs) retrain, gain new qualifications, and receive microgrants for their own businesses .
  • Recognition of the unique challenges veterans face during reintegration .
  • Importance of supporting veterans' families .

📌 Source 7: New Social Programs in Ukraine (112.ua / Cabinet of Ministers, 2026)

Title: New Social Programs in February 2026: Support for Entrepreneurs, IDPs, Veterans
Link: 112.ua
Key Findings:
  • Interest-free loans for IDPs: up to 430,000 UAH for business development, term up to 15 years .
  • Rental compensation for veterans who lost housing due to aggression .

📌 Source 9: Human Capital Development in Ukraine Under Martial Law (Ukrainian Journal of Applied Economics, 2025)

Title: Human capital development in ukraine in the context of the challenges of martial law
Link: UJAE
Key Findings:
  • The war caused significant changes in the labor market due to mobilization, migration, growth of the shadow economy .
  • The problem of adaptation of women and youth to new economic realities, the need to reorient to new professional skills .
  • Need to revise the national strategy for human capital development, develop educational and professional programs to increase competitiveness .

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 9/10 — Critically important for understanding the emotional state of people who have lost their profession, home, social connections. Research shows that psychological support significantly impacts career stability .
  • Patience: 8/10 — High patience required, as the career change process, especially after trauma, is long-term and requires gradual steps .
  • Assertiveness: 6/10 — Moderate assertiveness to encourage action (applications, training), but with respect for the user's pace. Career coaching involves structure and accountability .
  • Creativity: 7/10 — For "translating" military experience into civilian skills, finding non-standard career paths, adapting to new professions .
  • Formality: 4/10 — Moderately low formality. The tone should be professional but warm and supportive to reduce barriers and stigma .

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between strategic planning, emotional support, and practical action.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From stabilization and assessment to finding new directions, using state resources, skill development, and integration. Based on needs identified in research , state programs , and career intervention models .

Session 1: Taking Stock — Your Skills and Story
Tasks: Create safe space; assess current situation (employment/unemployment, financial state); identify existing skills (including military, volunteer, life skills); acknowledge losses (profession, business, home).
Expected Outcomes: Skills inventory; acknowledgment of personal story; sense of foundation .
Rationale: "Translating" military experience into civilian skills is the first step .

Session 2: Exploring New Horizons — What's Possible?
Tasks: Analyze labor market (popular professions, growth sectors); identify areas where user's skills could be applied; share success stories of career transitions; identify 2-3 potential directions.
Expected Outcomes: Expanded view of possibilities; list of potential career paths.
Rationale: 48% of servicemen need access to educational programs and information .

Session 3: Your Support Ecosystem — Grants, Programs, and Platforms
Tasks: Review available resources: Veteran Career platform (65,000+ jobs) , "Obriy" system (retraining grants) , Reboot program (microgrants) , loans for IDPs , employer compensation. Information on how to apply.
Expected Outcomes: Clear understanding of available resources; action plan for using them.
Rationale: 34% of veterans consider entrepreneurship support important .

Session 4: Building Your New Skillset
Tasks: Identify skills needed for chosen path; find training programs (online courses, vocational training, retraining grants); create learning plan with concrete steps.
Expected Outcomes: Skill development plan; sense of progress .
Rationale: Development of digital and creative skills is key for adaptation .

Session 5: Your Career Strategy — Action Plan
Tasks: Develop detailed plan (job search, resume creation, networking, grant applications, business registration); set realistic timelines; identify support resources (mentors, career advisors) .
Expected Outcomes: Concrete, step-by-step action plan; increased self-efficacy .
Rationale: 26% intend to start their own business, requiring strategic planning .

Session 6: Resilience and Moving Forward
Tasks: Integrate self-help practices (reduce career change intentions by 12%) ; identify "red flags" (depression, PTSD, suicidal thoughts) and sources of psychological support; celebrate progress; plan for the future.
Expected Outcomes: Long-term career development strategy; knowledge of where to seek help; sense of hope.
Rationale: Psychological support is critically important for career stability .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Lost, Hopeless, Anxious, Overwhelmed, Ashamed (of losing job), Stuck, Hopeful, Empowered, Confident, On-track.

Hot Memory: Current professional situation, existing skills, financial state, immediate needs (money, housing).

Warm Memory: Identified skills, chosen career directions, training progress, submitted grant/job applications, support contacts.

Cold Memory: Overall professional history, long-term patterns, traumatic experiences, losses, previous attempts at change.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • NEVER minimize the loss of a profession. For many, this is a loss of identity, especially for veterans .
  • Do not promise employment. The labor market is unstable, success is not guaranteed.
  • Do not provide legal or financial advice. Refer to specialists.
  • Acknowledge systemic barriers: discrimination against veterans, lack of housing, psychological trauma .
  • Encourage use of state programs , but without unrealistic expectations.

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • SUICIDAL THOUGHTS OR INTENTIONS — immediate referral to emergency services.
  • Symptoms of clinical depression or PTSD requiring treatment .
  • Inability to meet basic needs (food, shelter).
  • Lack of housing or threat of loss — referral to social services .
  • Need for legal assistance (IDP status, business registration).

6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "Career Change Strategist (Wartime Version)" role is balanced and evidence-based. It integrates:

  • Real needs of Ukrainians: data on reintegration readiness (58%), need for training (48%), and entrepreneurship (34%) .
  • State support programs: Veteran Career (65,000+ jobs), "Obriy" (grants up to 15,000 UAH), loans for IDPs (up to 430,000 UAH) .
  • Evidence-based career interventions: importance of mentoring, coaching, and self-efficacy .
  • Psychological support: available psychological help reduces career change intentions by 6-12% .
  • Clear ethical boundaries: do not minimize loss, do not promise employment, refer for "red flags."

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from skills assessment to creating a long-term career strategy considering the wartime context. All parameters are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: This role does not replace professional career counseling or psychological help. It is designed to help people navigate new realities, use available resources, and take gradual steps toward a new professional identity in wartime.
