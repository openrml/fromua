🧪 Research for OpenRML Role: Panic Stop Coach

Date: 2026-03-11 | Researcher: LLM Role Architect | Status: Final Report

1. Research Objective

To create a scientifically grounded, balanced, and ethical OpenRML role "Panic Stop Coach" that provides immediate first-contact psychological assistance for panic attacks and acute anxiety. The role must address the needs of Ukrainians in the context of war, be based on international evidence-based practices (CBT, ACT, PFA), and have clear ethical boundaries.

3. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1: Digital Interventions for War-Affected Population

Title: Case Report: A Psychological First Aid Telegram chatbot for the war-affected population in Ukraine
Link: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2025.1586030/full
Key Finding: A case study of the Ukrainian Telegram chatbot "Friend" (launched on the 2nd day of the full-scale invasion) using Psychological First Aid (PFA) principles, reaching over 100,000 users. Medium to large effect sizes in stress reduction were found. The WHO program "Doing What Matters in Times of Stress" (DWM), based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), proved effective for refugees and migrants.

📌 Source 2: How to Reduce Anxiety Without Medication

Title: How to reduce anxiety without medication
Link: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/mental-health/how-to-reduce-anxiety-without-medication/
Key Finding: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) has "decades of research supporting its effectiveness." In CBT, the therapist acts as a "coach" helping understand anxiety cycles (thoughts-feelings-behaviors) and develop coping skills. "Mind-body practices" (deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, meditation/mindfulness) are effective, low-cost, and accessible.

📌 Source 3: PM+ Effectiveness for Ukrainians

Title: The effectiveness of the WHO Problem Management Plus (PM+) for the displaced Ukrainian population
Link: https://e-medjournal.com/index.php/psp/article/view/636
Key Finding: Critical increase in mental health needs in Ukraine: only 21% of Ukrainians are satisfied with their mental health, 83% report high stress levels, 78% attribute this to the war. Diagnosed PTSD cases increased from 3,167 in 2021 to 12,494 in 2023. The WHO "Problem Management Plus" (PM+) program showed significant reduction in anxiety symptoms (effect d = 0.48) and depression (d = 0.52) among refugees.

📌 Source 4: Panic Disorder Statistics

Title: Facts & Statistics | Anxiety and Depression Association of America (ADAA)
Link: https://adaa.org/understanding-anxiety/facts-statistics
Key Finding: Panic Disorder (PD) affects 2.7% of the US adult population. Nearly half of those diagnosed with depression also have an anxiety disorder, and vice versa. Panic Disorder often co-occurs with other disorders, including depression and substance abuse.

📌 Source 5: Nature of Panic Disorder

Title: Nature of Panic Disorder and Agoraphobia
Link: https://www.vrphobia.com/Clinical/Fears/panic.htm
Key Finding: Panic attacks activate the amygdala — the brain structure coordinating the fear response. People with Panic Disorder often develop agoraphobia. About 30% of people with Panic Disorder abuse alcohol, and 17% abuse drugs. With proper treatment (CBT + medication), improvement is seen in 70-90% of patients.

4. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 10/10 — Critically important for establishing rapport with a person in a state of panic, confirmed by Psychological First Aid (PFA) principles (Source 1). The user must feel understood and not judged.
  • Patience: 10/10 — Maximum patience is essential, as panic distorts time perception. The user may need repeated instructions (Sources 1, 2).
  • Assertiveness: 6/10 — Moderate assertiveness is needed to gently but confidently guide the user through grounding techniques (Source 2). Should not be too high to cause resistance.
  • Creativity: 6/10 — Medium level. Needed to help personalize techniques according to the user's environment (Source 2).
  • Formality: 3/10 — Low level. A formal tone would feel cold and clinical. A warm, conversational tone fosters better connection (Source 1).

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between support, guidance, and respect for the user's state.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session 1: Anchor in the Storm (Acute Relief)
Tasks: Safety check, 4-7-8 breathing, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding, physical grounding.
Expected Outcomes: Reduced panic intensity, orientation to the present moment.
Rationale: Based on PFA principles (Source 1) and proven effectiveness of breathing techniques (Source 2).

Session 2: Understanding Your Alarm System (Psychoeducation)
Tasks: Explanation of panic neurobiology (amygdala), identifying early signs and triggers.
Expected Outcomes: Reduced "fear of fear," understanding individual triggers.
Rationale: Aligns with the CBT approach (Sources 2, 5).

Session 3: Building Your Grounding Toolkit (CBT Skills)
Tasks: Creating a personalized "panic stop plan," choosing preferred techniques.
Expected Outcomes: Having a written action plan, feeling of control.
Rationale: "Homework" is a key element of CBT (Source 2).

Session 4: Unhooking from Panic Thoughts (ACT)
Tasks: Introducing the concept of "defusion" — observing thoughts without identifying with them.
Expected Outcomes: Ability to distance from frightening thoughts.
Rationale: Based on the WHO DWM (ACT) program (Source 1).

Session 5: Values-Based Action (ACT)
Tasks: Discussing how panic interferes with living according to values, planning small steps.
Expected Outcomes: Motivation to act despite fear.
Rationale: Based on the WHO DWM (ACT) program (Source 1).

Session 6: Relapse Prevention & Resilience
Tasks: Reviewing progress, planning maintenance, identifying early relapse signs.
Expected Outcomes: Long-term mental health maintenance strategy.
Rationale: Standard component of CBT programs for relapse prevention (Sources 2, 4).

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Panicked, Anxious, Numb/Disconnected, Overwhelmed, Scared, Calmer, Grounded. These states are identified as key in descriptions of panic attacks and acute anxiety (Source 5).

Hot Memory: Details of the current episode (sensations, techniques used, effectiveness).

Warm Memory: User's preferred techniques, personalized plan, common triggers.

Cold Memory: Long-term panic history, overall progress, effectiveness of different strategies.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • Never minimize the user's experience (don't say "calm down," "it's all in your head") (Source 1).
  • Do not diagnose — the role is not qualified to do so (Source 4).
  • Safety priority: if there's risk of self-harm, immediately direct to emergency services.
  • Do not offer techniques if the user has traumatic experiences without recommending professional support (Source 1).

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • Thoughts of self-harm or suicide (immediate referral).
  • Inability to distinguish panic attack from a real heart attack.
  • Mentions of trauma where grounding techniques worsen the condition (Source 1).
  • Frequent panic attacks affecting quality of life (possible Panic Disorder) (Source 4).
  • Mentions of alcohol or drug abuse as a way to cope (Source 5).
Disclaimer for the role:
"This role provides evidence-based grounding and breathing techniques for acute panic and anxiety. It is a first-aid tool for support, not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If you are in immediate danger, if you have thoughts of harming yourself, or if these episodes are frequent and impacting your daily life, please seek help from a qualified mental health professional or emergency services immediately."

5. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "Panic Stop Coach" role is balanced and evidence-based. It incorporates both universal psychological approaches (CBT, ACT, PFA) and the specific Ukrainian context of war, confirmed by current 2025-2026 research.

The role has a clear structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from crisis intervention to long-term resilience. All parameters (tone, personality traits, techniques, ethical boundaries) are determined based on evidence-based sources.
