Remote Work Wellness Under Stress: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Remote Work Wellness Coach" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainians working remotely during wartime — power outages, working in shelters, constant anxiety, unstable internet.

Remote work, which offers flexibility and freedom, becomes an additional source of stress during wartime. When your home becomes a bomb shelter and your workspace is a corner in a basement, the boundaries between professional and personal life completely blur. Research confirms: the less clear the boundaries between work and home, the higher the risk of fatigue, burnout, and stress [1].

Key Challenges of Remote Work in Extreme Conditions

🔹 Blurred Boundaries
When the laptop is always at hand and notifications never stop, the brain constantly remains in "work mode," not allowing the body to rest [2].
🔹 Social Isolation
Lack of in-person meetings with colleagues, especially when you've been forced to relocate or are isolated for safety, intensifies feelings of loneliness [3].
🔹 Digital Fatigue
Constant video calls and messages lead to emotional exhaustion and reduced concentration [4].
🔹 Overtime Work
Without a clear "time to go home" signal, people work longer, skip lunch breaks, and don't take pauses [5].
🔹 Technical Issues
Unstable internet, connection problems, power outages — all this creates additional frustration.

Strategies for Supporting Well-being

🔹 Clear Boundaries and Routine
Setting fixed working hours, creating a "digital sunset" (e.g., turning off notifications at 7 PM), and physically separating the work zone [6].
🔹 Microbreaks
Short 5-minute pauses every hour to stand up, stretch, look out the window. This reduces eye strain and refreshes the mind [7].
🔹 Transition Rituals
Instead of the lost commute, create new rituals: morning walk, changing clothes, "breadcrumb" (writing down the first task for tomorrow) [8].
🔹 Physical Activity
Even a short walk during the day resets the nervous system and improves mood [9].
🔹 Communication
Consciously creating opportunities for informal communication with colleagues, online "coffee breaks" [10].
🔹 Autonomy and Support
It's important to have control over your schedule and feel support from leadership that doesn't disturb you after hours [11].

Practical Techniques for Remote Work Under Stress

📅 Daily Planning:
  • Identify 3 main tasks for the day (no more)
  • Schedule complex tasks during peak energy hours
  • Set clear start and end times for work
  • Account for possible power/internet outages
🧘 Grounding Techniques During Work:
  • 4-7-8 breathing: before a difficult call or when anxious
  • 5-4-3-2-1: if you feel panic during an air raid
  • "Body scan": 2 minutes to notice tension
🏡 Workspace Organization:
  • Even in a basement, try to create a "work corner"
  • Use noise-canceling headphones
  • Keep a portable power bank for devices
  • Have a Plan B for outages (mobile internet)

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Inability to disconnect from work even at night
  • Constant guilt about "not working enough"
  • Panic attacks before work calls
  • Complete loss of motivation and interest in work
  • Thoughts of self-harm due to work stress
