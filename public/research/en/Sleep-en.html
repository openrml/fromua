Sleep Hygiene in Extreme Conditions: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Sleep Hygiene Coach" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainians who have to sleep in shelters, with sirens, and under constant stress.

Scale of the Problem: Studies among refugees show sleep disturbance prevalence ranging from 32.6% to 73.3% [1], significantly higher than the 11.3% in the general population [2]. War creates unique obstacles to sleep: stress, anxiety, loss of home, and physical conditions — shelters, sirens, noise, lack of a familiar bed.

Main Factors Disrupting Sleep in Extreme Conditions

Military context research identifies five key environmental obstacles to sleep [3]:

  1. Light — disrupts circadian rhythms, suppresses melatonin production
  2. Noise — sirens, sounds of shelling, shelter chatter
  3. Temperature — too hot or too cold
  4. Polluted air — in shelters, basements
  5. Uncomfortable sleeping surface — hard floor, lack of mattress

Important: light and noise are the easiest to mitigate [4]. Advice should focus on these.

What is Sleep Hygiene?

Sleep hygiene is the daily habits and environmental factors that support healthy sleep [5]. WHO recommends 7-9 hours of quality sleep per night for adults [6].

Evidence-Based Interventions for Improving Sleep

For people who have experienced trauma and forced displacement, the following have proven effective [7]:

  • Psychoeducation — explaining that sleep disturbances after trauma are normal
  • Music relaxation
  • Guided imagery
  • Nightmare therapy

Practical Tips for Sleep in Extreme Conditions

According to WHO recommendations [8] and general sleep hygiene principles:

✅ Regular schedule — go to bed and wake up at the same time as much as possible [9]. This helps set your internal clock.
✅ Combat light — even in a shelter you can create darkness: eye mask, dark cloth, cap. Light suppresses melatonin production.
✅ Combat noise — earplugs, headphones, white noise (apps with rain, wind, "fan noise") [10].
✅ Pre-sleep ritual — even 10 minutes of calm activity: breathing, light stretching, listening to calm music. This signals the brain to prepare for sleep.
🧘 Relaxation techniques for falling asleep:
  • 4-7-8 breathing: inhale 4 sec, hold 7 sec, exhale 8 sec — activates the parasympathetic nervous system [11]
  • Progressive muscle relaxation: tense and relax muscle groups from feet to head
  • Guided imagery: imagine a safe place, calm landscape
✅ Avoid screens — blue light from phones disrupts melatonin production. If possible, avoid phones an hour before sleep (or use "night mode").
✅ Body care — regular meals, physical activity (even a short walk during the day) improve sleep quality [12].
⚠️ Limit caffeine and alcohol — alcohol worsens sleep quality (disrupts deep sleep phases), although it may help with falling asleep [13]. Caffeine should be avoided after 2 PM.

Working with Nightmares

Nightmares are common after trauma [14]. What can help:

  • "Dream rewriting" technique — upon waking, imagine a different, safe ending to the dream
  • Grounding after waking — 5-4-3-2-1 technique (5 things I see, 4 hear, 3 feel, 2 smell, 1 taste)
  • Write down thoughts — if thoughts prevent sleep, write them down and "postpone" them until morning

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Daily panic attacks or nightmares
  • Inability to sleep for several days in a row
  • Thoughts of self-harm
  • Fear of falling asleep due to nightmares
  • Inability to get out of bed for weeks (sign of depression)
