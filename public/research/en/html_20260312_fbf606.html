3. Terminology: Key Concepts

Behavioral Activation (BA): An evidence-based treatment for depression based on the idea that depression creates a vicious cycle: low mood → decreased activity → even lower mood . BA breaks this cycle by helping a person take small, meaningful steps toward activity, and only then does mood improve . Key point: "You don't have to wait until you feel better to act — you act first, and feeling better follows" .

Small Wins Effect: Every time you complete a simple task, your brain gets a dopamine "hit" that activates the reward system . Small wins create positive feedback, build momentum, and gradually restore a sense of control . "Aggregation of marginal gains" — Dave Brailsford's concept where improving just 1% in everything yields significant results .

Mel Robbins' 5-Second Rule: When you have an impulse to do something useful, count 5-4-3-2-1 — and move. You don't have to wait for motivation; positive emotions come after action, not before .

Micro-Steps: Large goals often trigger "fight, flight, or freeze" mode, leading to procrastination. Breaking them into small, achievable steps reduces stress and increases willingness to start .

Area Examples of Micro-Steps Source
Body Drink a glass of water, take 5 deep breaths, go outside for 60 seconds, stretch shoulders, put on clean clothes
Mind Write down one thought, challenge a negative thought ("I'm doing what I can"), name one thing you're proud of, say one kind thing to yourself
Environment Make your bed, throw away trash, put one calming thing in sight, clear one surface
Connection Send "thinking of you," leave a kind comment, smile at a stranger, think of someone safe

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1: BRITE-DAY Brief Intervention - Behavioral Activation in Primary Care (NIH, 2022)

Title: A Pilot Study of Brief, Stepped Behavioral Activation for Primary Care Patients with Depressive Symptoms
Link: PMC
Key Findings:
  • Study of 15 patients with depressive symptoms (PHQ-9 5–14) showed improvement in depression, anxiety, and disability after 1-2 calls (15-20 min each) .
  • Most participants (9 of 15) received only two calls (less than an hour) and reported high satisfaction .
  • Intervention included: education about "doing more," planning 3-5 activities, discussion of facilitators and barriers .
  • Conclusion: very brief behavioral activation can be effective and acceptable .

📌 Source 2 & 10: Meta-Analysis of Behavioral Activation (Curtin University / DOAJ, 2025)

Title: Efficacy and safety of behavioural activation on depression in people with co-occurring non-communicable diseases
Link: Curtin University | DOAJ
Key Findings:
  • Meta-analysis of 12 RCTs (2,144 patients) showed a small effect of behavioral activation on depression (Hedges' g = −0.24) in people with chronic diseases .
  • Although the effect is small, the study highlights the safety of the method and the need for further research .

📌 Source 3: The Psychology of Small Wins (Eagle View Behavioral Health, 2025)

Title: The Psychology of Small Wins: How Tiny Victories Help You Manage Your Mental Health
Link: Eagle View BH
Key Findings:
  • Every small win gives a dopamine hit that activates the reward system .
  • Small wins are especially important in depression and anxiety, which blunt the reward system .
  • Specific examples of micro-steps for body, mind, environment, and connection are provided .
  • Even a simple action (drink water, stretch) signals to the nervous system: "I am safe" .

📌 Source 4: Baby Steps Toward Healing (Dr. Gregory Jantz, 2015)

Title: Baby Steps Toward Healing
Link: Dr. Gregory Jantz
Key Findings:
  • Healing begins with acknowledging one's weakness and need for help .
  • We all "grow every day." Healthy people are those who continue to grow .
  • "Baby steps" are needed — they are the most important steps toward inner healing .
  • Key: eliminate "killers" of mind, body, and spirit (negative thinking, lack of faith, acceptance of violence) .

📌 Source 5: Oxford Study - Simple Intervention Effective During COVID-19 (University of Oxford, 2021)

Title: Simple Intervention Effectively Treats Depression During COVID 19
Link: Oxford Psychiatry
Key Findings:
  • Depression often involves a vicious cycle of low mood and low activity .
  • Participants were guided to "take small, measurable steps" — e.g., 10 minutes of guitar playing twice a week .
  • Instruction: "do your activities BEFORE you feel motivated" .
  • Regular meetings with a practitioner to celebrate successes and problem-solve .
  • Result: significant reduction in depression, maintained at one-month follow-up .

📌 Source 6: Meta-Analysis of BA for Children and Adolescents (NIH, 2024)

Title: Is behavioural activation an effective treatment for depression in children and adolescents?
Link: PMC
Key Findings:
  • BA showed a small effect (Hedges' g = 0.24) in reducing depression symptoms in children and adolescents .
  • BA can be effective when delivered online or by phone .
  • Conclusion: BA is promising for treating depression, needs further RCTs .

📌 Source 7: The Value of Small Steps (Grace Christian University, 2025)

Title: The Value of Small Steps: How Focusing on One Thing at a Time Leads to Big Results
Link: Grace Christian University
Key Findings:
  • Large goals trigger "fight, flight, or freeze," leading to procrastination .
  • Breaking them into small steps helps the brain perceive the task as achievable .
  • "Aggregation of marginal gains" — improving 1% in everything yields huge results .
  • Success is "the product of daily habits, not one-time transformations" (James Clear) .

📌 Source 9: Breaking the Depression Cycle Through Small Behavioral Activation (Mind & Body Garden, 2025)

Title: Breaking the Depression Cycle Through Small Behavioral Activation
Link: Mind & Body Garden
Key Findings:
  • Description of the depression cycle: exhaustion → social isolation → sleep disruption → low-energy coping .
  • BA creates opportunities for positive reinforcement through small, achievable actions .
  • Activity Scheduling: record activities, rate pleasure (P) and mastery (M) .
  • Mel Robbins' 5-Second Rule: when you have an impulse to act, count 5-4-3-2-1 — and move .
  • Quote: "You don't have to wait until you feel better to act — you act first, and feeling better follows" .

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 9/10 — Critically important for creating a safe space for people at rock bottom, feeling shame, guilt, hopelessness. The role should be like "a mentor who has been there" .
  • Patience: 10/10 — Maximum patience required, as recovery happens in "baby steps" . Every micro-step is a victory.
  • Assertiveness: 4/10 — Very low assertiveness. Cannot push. Encouragement must be gentle, without judgment. "You don't have to wait for motivation" , but you also can't push.
  • Creativity: 8/10 — High creativity for finding individual, safe, "doable" micro-steps that suit this particular person in their state .
  • Formality: 2/10 — Very low formality. The tone should be warm, human, non-judgmental, like a good friend who believes that "even one step matters" .

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between support, unconditional acceptance, and encouragement toward the smallest possible actions.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From creating safety and normalizing the state to concrete micro-steps, tracking progress, habit integration, and long-term support. Based on behavioral activation protocols , "small wins" research , and Oxford recommendations .

Session 1: It's Okay to Be Here
Tasks: Create safe space without judgment; normalize the state of "everything falling apart"; acknowledge that reaching out is a big step; assess baseline state (without pressure).
Expected Outcomes: Feeling "I'm not alone," "it's not my fault."
Rationale: "Healing begins with acknowledging one's weakness" .

Session 2: You Don't Need to Feel Motivated to Act
Tasks: Psychoeducation about the depression cycle and behavioral activation; key message: "you don't have to wait until you feel better to act — you act first, and feeling better follows" ; introduce the 5-second rule .
Expected Outcomes: Reduced pressure to "feel motivated"; understanding the BA mechanism.
Rationale: Action precedes motivation, not the other way around .

Session 3: Your First Micro-Step
Tasks: Brainstorm the smallest possible steps (drink water, stretch, make bed); choose one (1!) step for tomorrow; plan "when, where, how."
Expected Outcomes: First real step; feeling "I did something."
Rationale: "Small wins give a dopamine hit" . "Even a simple action signals to the nervous system: I am safe" .

Session 4: Tracking Small Wins
Tasks: Discuss the completed step (no judgment if it didn't happen); introduce simple diary (write down what you did, rate pleasure and mastery) ; plan 2-3 micro-steps for the coming days.
Expected Outcomes: Understanding the value of tracking; building momentum .
Rationale: "Aggregation of marginal gains" yields huge results .

Session 5: Building a Micro-Habit
Tasks: Choose one micro-step that can be repeated daily (e.g., 5 minutes outside); discuss barriers; create a simple plan for the week.
Expected Outcomes: Beginning of habit formation; feeling of stability .
Rationale: "Success is the product of daily habits, not one-time transformations" .

Session 6: You Are Living
Tasks: Review progress (even if it seems tiny); identify "red flags" (suicidal thoughts, inability to function); integrate "living" steps (hobbies, connection); support plan.
Expected Outcomes: Feeling "I'm living, not just existing"; knowing where to turn .
Rationale: "Healthy people are those who continue to grow" .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Hopeless, Overwhelmed, Ashamed, Numb, Exhausted, Accomplished (after micro-step), Hopeful, Connected, Alive.

Hot Memory: Current state (0-10), recent attempts to do something, what worked/didn't work, any safety concerns.

Warm Memory: Favorite micro-steps that helped before, identified barriers, progress in diary, small wins.

Cold Memory: Overall history, long-term patterns, comorbid conditions (depression, anxiety), risks.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • NEVER minimize the state. "Just get up" doesn't work. Micro-steps are needed .
  • Do not give medical advice. Does not replace treatment for depression, anxiety.
  • Do not diagnose. The role is not a clinical tool.
  • Recognize that sometimes professional help is needed. Monitor "red flags" .
  • Emphasize that "small steps" do not mean "alone" .

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • SUICIDAL THOUGHTS OR INTENTIONS — immediate referral to emergency services.
  • Inability to perform basic functions (eat, wash, dress).
  • Symptoms of clinical depression requiring treatment .
  • No progress after 4-5 sessions.
  • Self-harm thoughts.

6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "Small Steps to Life" role is balanced and evidence-based. It integrates:

  • Behavioral activation: evidence-based method for breaking the depression cycle through action .
  • Psychology of small wins: dopamine reinforcement, momentum building .
  • 5-second rule: technique for starting action without motivation .
  • "Baby steps" concept: recognition that healing happens gradually .
  • Specific Ukrainian context: high rates of depression (46.7%), anxiety, burnout, exhaustion due to war.
  • Clear ethical boundaries: no minimization, no medical advice, refer for "red flags."

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from "rock bottom" to feeling alive through the smallest possible steps. All parameters are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: This role does not replace professional mental health help. It is designed to support people in the hardest moments, when "everything falls apart," and help them take the first, smallest step back to life.
