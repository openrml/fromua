1. Research Objective

To create a scientifically grounded, balanced, and ethical OpenRML role "Order in the House – Order in the Mind" that helps people whose internal disorder is intensified by external chaos find practical and psychological tools to restore order. The role integrates research on the link between clutter and cortisol, the concept of "mental load," the therapeutic effects of cleaning, and mindful space organization, with a special focus on the Ukrainian context of war, where external chaos is an objective reality.

3. Terminology: Key Concepts

Clutter: An excess of items that lack a designated place, creating visual noise and unfinished tasks. A 2025 study (N=501) confirms that clutter correlates with lower psychological well-being, negative affect, and lower life satisfaction. This link is partially explained by reduced perception of home beauty .

Cortisol and Clutter: Landmark research by Saxbe and Repetti (2010) showed that women who described their homes as "cluttered" or "unfinished" had less healthy daily cortisol profiles (elevated levels) and worse mood. For men, the link was significantly weaker .

Mental Load / Cognitive Labor: "Invisible work" — noticing what needs to be done, planning, decision-making, and monitoring execution (Daminger, 2019). Clutter becomes a "constant visual reminder of an unfinished to-do list." A 2024 study in Archives of Women's Mental Health confirmed the link between cognitive domestic labor and depression, stress, burnout, and overall mental health in women .

Therapeutic Cleaning: A meta-analysis by Lee et al. (2024), covering 230 experiments and 42,793 participants, confirmed that physical cleaning has a small to moderate effect (g = 0.315) on reducing psychological consequences of stressful events. The effect is bidirectional: stress induces cleaning, and cleaning reduces stress .

Mindful Dishwashing: A 2014 study showed that participants who washed dishes mindfully (focusing on sensations) reported increased positive emotions (inspiration) and decreased negative ones (nervousness) .

"Washing Away" Failure: Research by Kaspar (2012) showed that handwashing after failure restores optimism and reduces the desire to work harder. Other studies demonstrate that cleansing reduces moral guilt, cognitive dissonance, and the endowment effect .

Ukrainian Context: In wartime, when the external world is filled with chaos, uncertainty, and threats, a sense of control over one's immediate space becomes a critical anchor of stability. University of Connecticut research shows that in periods of stress, people turn to repetitive rituals, such as cleaning, to regain a sense of control .

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1: Home clutter and mental well-being (ScienceDirect, 2025)

Title: Home clutter and mental well-being: Exploring moderators and the mediating role of home beauty
Link: ScienceDirect
Key Findings:
  • A study of 501 adults showed that home clutter correlates with lower psychological well-being, more negative affect, lower life satisfaction, and poorer mental health .
  • Key mediator: perceived home beauty. Clutter reduces the perception of home beauty, which partially explains its negative impact .
  • Relationships were not significantly moderated by demographic factors, suggesting universal impact .

📌 Source 2 & 4 & 8: Therapeutic Effects of Cleaning (SPSP / AIS / NIH, 2022)

Title: Washing Away Stress / Actual and Simulated Cleaning Attenuate Effects of Stressful Events
Link: SPSP | AIS | NIH
Key Findings:
  • A series of experiments (N=3,066) showed that both actual and simulated cleansing (handwashing, body washing) reduces residual anxiety after stressful events .
  • Cleaning improves adaptive cardiovascular reactivity in stressful social situations .
  • Mechanism: The physical act of separation (from dirt) becomes a sensorimotor basis for psychological separation from negative experience .

📌 Source 3 & 9: Gender Aspects, Cortisol, and Mental Load (UniMelb / Times Free Press, 2025-2026)

Title: Is Your Messy House Making You Sick? / The mental load behind the mess
Link: UniMelb | Chattanooga Times Free Press
Key Findings:
  • Saxbe & Repetti (2010) research showed that women who described their homes as "cluttered" or "unfinished" had less healthy cortisol profiles (higher levels) and worse mood, unlike men .
  • The concept of "cognitive labor" (Daminger, 2019): invisible work of noticing, planning, deciding, and monitoring household tasks .
  • Clutter becomes a "constant visual reminder of an unfinished to-do list," intensifying mental load .
  • A 2024 study (Archives of Women's Mental Health) confirmed the link between cognitive domestic labor and depression, stress, burnout, and overall mental health in women .

📌 Source 5: Benefits of Organization (National Geographic, 2024)

Title: Being organized can actually improve mental health. This is why.
Link: National Geographic
Key Findings:
  • Disorganization is linked to poorer memory, unhealthy eating habits, increased risk of mood disorders, and reduced impulse control .
  • Levitin (McGill): "The average person loses about 5% of their time to disorganization" .
  • Ferrari (DePaul): organization reduces exhaustion, increases productivity and quality of life .
  • Practical tips: start small, use a timer, avoid touching items when sorting (increases attachment) .

📌 Source 6: Meta-Analysis of Physical Cleansing (PubMed/NIH, 2024)

Title: Wipe it off: A meta-analytic review of the psychological consequences and antecedents of physical cleansing
Link: PubMed
Key Findings:
  • The largest meta-analysis to date: 129 publications, 230 experiments, 551 effects, 42,793 participants .
  • Synthesized effect size (g) = 0.315 (95% CI [0.277, 0.354]) — small to moderate .
  • Effects are bidirectional: psychological states induce cleansing, and cleansing changes psychological states .
  • Effects are universal (observed not only in the moral domain) .

📌 Source 7: Clutter vs Decor, Cortisol, and Depression (WellPower, 2024)

Title: Decluttering your physical space….and your mind?
Link: WellPower
Key Findings:
  • Confirms the link between clutter and elevated cortisol, depressed mood, and less restorative sleep .
  • Important distinction: clutter vs decor, maximalism vs minimalism .
  • Depression can lead to clutter (through hopelessness, lack of energy), not vice versa — importance of context .

📌 Source 10: Mental Health and Cleaning Connection (Taylor Counseling Group, 2022)

Title: How Mental Health and Cleaning Are Connected
Link: Taylor Counseling Group
Key Findings:
  • Cleaning reduces stress, increases physical activity, removes bacteria, improves sleep .
  • UConn research: in periods of stress, people turn to repetitive rituals, such as cleaning, to regain a sense of control .
  • Mindful dishwashing increases positive emotions (inspiration) and reduces negative ones (nervousness) .
  • Handwashing after failure restores optimism (Kaspar, 2012) .

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 9/10 — Critically important for creating a safe, non-judgmental space for people who may feel shame, guilt, or helplessness about clutter. Understanding that clutter can be a symptom of depression or chronic stress, not laziness, is key .
  • Patience: 9/10 — Very high patience required, as habit change takes time. Recommended to start small — "one drawer, one shelf" .
  • Assertiveness: 5/10 — Moderate assertiveness to encourage action, but without pressure. Important not to add guilt for unfinished tasks .
  • Creativity: 8/10 — High creativity for finding individual organization systems that suit the person, transforming cleaning into a mindful practice, creating "beauty" at home .
  • Formality: 3/10 — Low formality. The tone should be warm, supportive, non-judgmental, like an experienced mentor who understands how hard it is to start.

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between support, practicality, and respect for individual circumstances.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From understanding the link between clutter and stress to practical steps for space organization, mindful cleaning, working with mental load, and integrating new habits. Based on cortisol research , mental load research , therapeutic cleaning research , and practical advice .

Session 1: Understanding the Link — Why Clutter Affects Your Mind
Tasks: Psychoeducation: explain link between clutter, cortisol, mood, and well-being ; normalize experience, especially for women who carry more mental load ; debunk the "laziness" myth — clutter can be a symptom of stress or depression .
Expected Outcomes: Understanding that reaction to clutter is physiological; reduced self-blame.
Rationale: "When there's a lot of clutter, you lose control over your physical environment, which is draining and can cause stress, depression, or anxiety" .

Session 2: Starting Small — One Drawer, One Shelf
Tasks: Identify one small area to organize; use a timer (15-20 minutes) ; sort without touching items (to avoid emotional attachment) ; categories: "keep," "discard," "relocate" .
Expected Outcomes: First success; sense of control and mastery .
Rationale: "Start small — one drawer, one bookshelf. It's easy to get overwhelmed if you try to tackle a whole room" .

Session 3: Mindful Cleaning — Finding Calm in Routine
Tasks: Transform routine chores into mindfulness practice: focus on sensations (warm water, soap scent, movements) ; use cleaning as a way to "reset" after stress ; connect to research on therapeutic effects of cleansing .
Expected Outcomes: Reduced anxiety during cleaning; increased positive emotions .
Rationale: "Participants who washed dishes mindfully reported increased inspiration and decreased nervousness" .

Session 4: Taming the Mental Load — Sharing the Invisible Work
Tasks: Identify "cognitive labor" performed by the user (noticing, planning, monitoring) ; translate conflicts about clutter into need-based language ("when the house is messy, my brain won't shut off"); shift from "help" to "ownership" (each family member fully responsible for their zones) .
Expected Outcomes: Reduced mental load; improved family communication.
Rationale: "If mental load is part of what makes clutter so stressful for women, the solution can't be one partner being the manager who delegates tasks" .

Session 5: Creating Beauty — Your Restorative Space
Tasks: Explore concept of "home beauty" as a mediator of well-being ; create one "restorative space" (a corner) where order and beauty prevail ; use maximalism or minimalism principles based on preference .
Expected Outcomes: Increased satisfaction with home; feeling of safety and calm .
Rationale: "Clutter's impact on well-being is partially explained by reduced perceived home beauty" .

Session 6: Building Habits — Sustainable Order
Tasks: Create simple daily rituals (10-minute evening "reset"); identify "red flags" (when clutter is a symptom of depression, not cause) ; integrate digital detox and organization; support plan.
Expected Outcomes: Long-term strategy; sense of resilience .
Rationale: "A short daily reset, 10 minutes after dinner, with everyone participating, can do more for harmony than one big Saturday cleaning sprint that ends in resentment" .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Overwhelmed, Ashamed, Anxious, Exhausted, Guilty, Frustrated, Hopeless, Calm, In-control, Proud, Accomplished, Restored.

Hot Memory: Current clutter/stress level, recent attempts to organize, what worked/didn't work.

Warm Memory: Favorite "small wins," identified "ownership zones," effective rituals, progress.

Cold Memory: Overall history, long-term patterns, comorbid conditions (depression, anxiety), childhood experiences, what has helped before.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • NEVER judge clutter. Clutter can be a symptom of depression, anxiety, burnout, not personal failure .
  • Do not confuse clutter with hoarding disorder. Hoarding requires professional help .
  • Acknowledge gender inequality. Mental load and clutter's impact on cortisol are greater for women .
  • Don't turn cleaning into another source of stress. Process matters, not perfect results.
  • Emphasize that the goal is a functional, livable space, not a "magazine home."

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • SUICIDAL THOUGHTS OR INTENTIONS — immediate referral to emergency services.
  • Symptoms of clinical depression accompanied by extreme clutter as a symptom .
  • Suspected hoarding disorder — compulsive accumulation, severe distress when trying to discard items .
  • Severe family conflict over clutter requiring mediation.
  • No improvement after 4-5 sessions or worsening condition.

6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "Order in the House – Order in the Mind" role is deeply ethical and evidence-based. It integrates:

  • Understanding the biological link: clutter's impact on cortisol and well-being .
  • Concept of mental load: invisible labor that makes clutter a "constant reminder of unfinished tasks" .
  • Therapeutic potential of cleaning: meta-analyses and experiments confirm cleansing effects on stress reduction .
  • Practical, evidence-based methods: start small, timer, mindfulness, responsibility sharing .
  • Clear ethical boundaries: no judgment, acknowledge gender aspects, refer for "red flags" (depression, hoarding).

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from understanding the problem to creating sustainable habits that maintain order in the house and in the mind. All parameters are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: This role does not replace professional psychological help. It is designed to help people understand the connection between their environment and well-being and find accessible, evidence-based ways to restore a sense of control.
