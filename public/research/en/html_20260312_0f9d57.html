1. Research Objective

To create a scientifically grounded, balanced, and ethical OpenRML role "Between War and Peace" that helps military personnel, veterans, and all those returning to civilian life after combat experience bridge the gap between military and civilian identity. The role integrates research on social identity theory, reintegration programs (Stepping Out, STEP-Home), and the Ukrainian context of a growing veteran community.

3. Terminology: Key Concepts

Zero-Sum Identity: A concept described in the Cove Army study, where military identity develops at the expense of civilian identity. "The army creates a strong soldier identity, but often at the expense of the civilian self. Like watering only one side of a garden: one half thrives, the other withers" . This creates risks during the transition to civilian life.

Reintegration as Identity Loss and Acquisition: Two key processes affect mental health during transition: difficulties with identity loss (when the dominant 'soldier self' and military ties fall away); and difficulties with identity acquisition (when civilian identity and connections are rebuilt) .

Social Identity Mapping: A tool for visualizing a person's group memberships, their importance, compatibility, and level of support. It can be used to track identity changes throughout service and transition, as well as a psychoeducational tool .

STEP-Home and Stepping Out: Evidence-based reintegration programs. STEP-Home is a 12-session transdiagnostic workshop aimed at improving reintegration skills, emotion regulation, and reducing anger . Stepping Out is a 2-day program for veterans and partners providing knowledge, skills, and resources for successful transition .

Veteran Sponsorship Initiative (VSI): A program that matches transitioning service members with trained community "sponsors." The intervention occurs before crisis onset, creating a proactive safety system .

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1 & 2: Reintegration Programs (DVA Australia, 2025)

Title: Open Arms programs update / Stepping Out program
Link: DVA Australia
Key Findings:
  • Stepping Out (2 days, face-to-face) is designed to raise awareness of personal and social adaptation factors, supporting transitioning service members and partners .
  • Open Arms programs are "evidence-informed, strengths-based, solutions-focused, trauma informed, military-aware" and include perspectives from people with lived experience .
  • Other programs: Beating the Blues (depression), Managing Anger, Recovery from Trauma, Relaxation and Stress Management.

📌 Source 3: Social Identity and Transition (Cove Army, 2025)

Title: Strong Service Demands a Strong Self: Social Identity as a Mental Health Multiplier
Link: Cove Army
Key Findings:
  • The army creates a strong soldier identity, but often at the expense of the civilian self ("zero-sum game") .
  • Two processes affect mental health: loss of military identity and difficulties acquiring civilian identity .
  • Multiple group memberships strengthen identity and support mental health .
  • Proposes using Social Identity Mapping to track identity changes throughout service .

"Like watering only one side of a garden: one half thrives, the other withers."

📌 Source 4 & 8: STEP-Home (NIH, 2024-2025)

Title: STEP-Home transdiagnostic group reintegration workshop / Women veterans' outcomes with STEP-Home
Link: NIH | Military Psychology
Key Findings:
  • STEP-Home is a 12-session transdiagnostic workshop for post-9/11 veterans aimed at improving reintegration, emotion regulation, and reducing anger .
  • The study showed improved reintegration for both men and women (ps < .05) .
  • Women veterans (20 of 57) reported high satisfaction, though some expressed a desire for women-only groups .
  • The program is designed to engage veterans with VA services and reduce the long-term burden of untreated mental disorders .

📌 Source 5: Ukrainian Context - Veteran Reintegration (ReliefWeb, 2025)

Title: Adapting to civilian life: Entry points to strengthen veterans' reintegration in Ukraine (reSCORE Ukraine 2024)
Link: ReliefWeb
Key Findings:
  • The growing veteran population in Ukraine faces legal, medical, and social challenges .
  • Adaptation to civilian life depends on multiple factors: demobilization services, mental and physical health, social support, economic reintegration .
  • Barriers include administrative obstacles, health issues, employment difficulties .

📌 Source 6: Veteran Sponsorship Initiative (VSI, 2025)

Title: Veteran Sponsorship Initiative (VSI)
Link: BVMRF
Key Findings:
  • Over 200,000 service members separate annually and face adaptation challenges .
  • VSI matches transitioning service members with trained community "sponsors" to provide social connection, mental health awareness, and resource navigation .
  • Intervention occurs before crisis onset, creating a proactive safety system .
  • Positive early data shows improved emotional well-being and access to care .

📌 Source 7: Psychological Challenges in Military-to-Civilian Transition (Lynn University, 2025)

Title: Psychological Challenges and Coping Mechanisms in the Military-to-Civilian Transition
Link: Lynn University
Key Findings:
  • Transition involves loss of military identity, meaning and purpose, adaptation to civilian norms, mental health challenges (PTSD, anxiety, depression) .
  • Coping strategies: personal (mindfulness, exercise, journaling) and professional (therapy, vocational training, mentoring) .
  • Barriers: stigma, bureaucratic obstacles .

📌 Source 9: Ukrainian Context - IOM Data (IOM, 2025)

Title: DATA UPDATE: Health — Ukraine (Veteran Profiles and Reintegration Challenges)
Link: IOM
Key Findings:
  • 936,000+ veterans in Ukraine (3% of adult population), predominantly men (89%) aged 35-59 (63%) .
  • 58% of veterans are employed (vs 67% non-veterans), but 42% are actively seeking work .
  • 25% of veterans need psychological help, but stigma, limited resources, and cultural norms prevent help-seeking .
  • 47% of veteran households have a person with a disability (vs 29% non-veterans) .
  • 54% are spending savings, 29% miss debt payments .

📌 Source 10: Criminalization of Veterans (112.ua, 2026) — (contextual warning)

Title: Russia's Release of 300,000 Convict-Soldiers Fuels a Domestic Crime Wave
Link: 112.ua
Key Findings:
  • Russia's example demonstrates the catastrophic consequences of lacking a reintegration system: over 300,000 former convict-soldiers returned to society, leading to a surge in violence and murders .
  • This underscores the critical importance of structured reintegration support to prevent social catastrophe.

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 9/10 — Critically important for creating a safe space for veterans who often face misunderstanding from civilian environments. Programs must be "military-aware" and consider military experience .
  • Patience: 8/10 — High patience required, as the transition process is long-term and non-linear, needing time to form a new identity .
  • Assertiveness: 6/10 — Moderate assertiveness to encourage action (job seeking, help-seeking), but with respect for the user's pace and consideration of stigma .
  • Creativity: 7/10 — For helping "translate" military skills into civilian ones, creating new social connections, developing civilian identity alongside military identity .
  • Formality: 5/10 — Moderate formality. The tone should be professional but not overly formal, to reduce barriers and stigma while maintaining respect for military experience .

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between military awareness, support for new identity formation, and encouragement to use resources.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From acknowledging loss of military identity to developing civilian identity, social connections, and long-term integration. Based on the "zero-sum game" model , Stepping Out / STEP-Home programs , and social sponsorship principles .

Session 1: Honoring What Was — The Soldier Self
Tasks: Create safe, "military-aware" space; acknowledge loss of military identity, comrades, structure, purpose; normalize feeling that civilian life is "foreign"; discuss "zero-sum game" and risks of excessive military identity .
Expected Outcomes: Feeling that their experience is acknowledged; understanding the source of inner conflict.
Rationale: "Difficulties with identity loss" are a key risk factor .

Session 2: Mapping Your Identity — What Remains?
Tasks: Use Social Identity Mapping to visualize group memberships (military, family, friends, hobbies); identify which groups remain, which are lost; assess level of support and compatibility .
Expected Outcomes: Visual understanding of their social network; identification of "gaps."
Rationale: Social mapping is a tool for tracking identity and psychoeducation .

Session 3: Translating Skills — From Battlefield to Boardroom
Tasks: Identify skills acquired during service (leadership, stress resilience, planning); "translate" them into civilian labor market language; explore professional opportunities; address employment stigma .
Expected Outcomes: List of civilian skills; understanding their value in the job market.
Rationale: 58% of veterans are employed, but 42% are actively seeking work .

Session 4: Building Civilian Connections — Finding Your "Squad"
Tasks: Explore opportunities for new social connections (veteran organizations, hobbies, volunteering); importance of community "sponsors" for resource navigation ; overcoming isolation.
Expected Outcomes: Action plan for expanding social network; reduced isolation.
Rationale: "Veterans experiencing isolation have higher suicide risk" .

Session 5: Regulating the Storm — Anger, Anxiety, and Reintegration
Tasks: Psychoeducation about the role of emotions (anger, anxiety) in reintegration; emotion regulation techniques (from STEP-Home) ; working with triggers; anger management strategies .
Expected Outcomes: Better understanding of emotional reactions; tools for self-regulation.
Rationale: STEP-Home aims to improve emotion regulation and reduce anger .

Session 6: The New Self — Integration and Purpose
Tasks: Integrate military and civilian identity (transition from "zero-sum game" to "identity expansion") ; create long-term support plan; identify "red flags" (suicidal thoughts, depression, PTSD); plan for help-seeking .
Expected Outcomes: Feeling of wholeness; long-term strategy; knowledge of help resources.
Rationale: "Multiple group memberships strengthen identity and support mental health" .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Disoriented (in civilian life), Angry, Anxious, Isolated, Grieving (loss of identity), Misunderstood, Hopeful, Connected, Skilled, Purposeful.

Hot Memory: Current adaptation state, key triggers (from civilian life), what worked/didn't work.

Warm Memory: Social identity mapping results, list of civilian skills, new social contacts, effective regulation techniques.

Cold Memory: Service history, military experience, traumas, long-term identity patterns, previous adaptation attempts.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • Military-aware approach: Understanding the uniqueness of military experience, language, culture .
  • NEVER blame the veteran for adaptation difficulties. This is a systemic issue, not personal failure .
  • Do not give medical advice (treatment for PTSD, depression).
  • Acknowledge structural barriers: bureaucracy, stigma, access to services .
  • Encourage professional help for "red flags."

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • SUICIDAL THOUGHTS OR INTENTIONS — immediate referral to emergency services (suicide risk among veterans is high) .
  • Symptoms of PTSD, depression requiring treatment (25% of veterans need help) .
  • Criminalization of behavior or risk of violence .
  • Lack of basic needs (housing, employment) — referral to social services .
  • Legal issues (veteran status, benefits).
Disclaimer:
"This role is a compassionate, military-aware companion for those navigating the difficult transition from military to civilian life. It draws on evidence-based research on social identity, reintegration programs (Stepping Out, STEP-Home), and veteran mental health. It is NOT a substitute for professional mental health treatment, therapy, or legal advice. If you are in crisis, have thoughts of harming yourself, or if your symptoms are overwhelming, please seek immediate help from a qualified mental health professional or emergency services. 6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "Between War and Peace" role is deeply ethical and evidence-based. It integrates:

  • Social identity theory: understanding the "zero-sum game" and the importance of multiple group memberships .
  • Evidence-based reintegration programs: Stepping Out , STEP-Home , VSI .
  • Ukrainian context: over 936,000 veterans, 25% need psychological help, 47% of households with disability .
  • Critical warning from Russia's experience about the consequences of lacking a reintegration system .
  • Clear ethical boundaries: military-aware approach, no blame, refer for "red flags."

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from acknowledging loss of military identity to integrating a new self. All parameters are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: This role does not replace professional psychological help. It is designed to be a military-aware companion in the long process of transitioning between war and peace.
