Hypervigilance and How to Manage It: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Hypervigilance Manager" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainians experiencing war — veterans, civilians in combat zones, people living under shelling for extended periods.

Hypervigilance is a state of heightened alertness, constantly scanning the environment for potential threats [1]. It is one of the most common reactions to trauma, especially in veterans, people who have experienced combat, or those who have lived in dangerous environments for extended periods [2].

How Hypervigilance Works

Hypervigilance arises from hyperactivity of the sympathetic nervous system [3]. The brain constantly remains in "fight or flight" mode, even in safe environments [4]. This is physically and emotionally exhausting — the body wasn't designed for prolonged stress [5].

Symptoms of Hypervigilance:

  • Constant feeling of anxiety and danger
  • Heightened reaction to unexpected sounds (startling)
  • Muscle tension, rapid heartbeat
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Sleep disturbances
  • Avoiding crowded places
  • Need to sit with back to the wall, monitor exits

Why Recognizing Safety Matters

The key task is to teach the nervous system to distinguish between real and perceived danger. Through Stephen Porges' Polyvagal Theory, we understand that hypervigilance is a protective mechanism that once helped survival but now interferes with living [6]. Neuroception — the brain's ability to assess safety — can be disrupted after trauma [7].

Strategies for Reducing Hypervigilance

Therapeutic Approaches:

  • EMDR — helps reprocess traumatic memories [8]
  • Somatic Experiencing — working with bodily sensations [9]
  • Trauma-Focused Therapy

Self-Help Techniques:

🔹 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding (eyes remain open) [10]:
  • 5 things I can see
  • 4 things I can touch
  • 3 things I can hear
  • 2 things I can smell
  • 1 thing I can taste
🔹 Breathing Techniques [11]:
  • Square breathing: inhale 4 sec — hold 4 — exhale 4 — hold 4
  • 4-7-8 breathing: inhale 4 sec, hold 7 sec, exhale 8 sec — activates parasympathetic system
🔹 Attention Shifting:
  • From worst to best in the present moment
  • Situational refocusing: "I'm overly focused on... shifting attention to..."
🔹 Sensory Techniques [12]:
  • Focus on sounds: listen to and list all sounds around
  • Focus on textures: touch different surfaces, describe them
  • Temperature grounding: cold/warm water
🔹 Physical Activity [13]:
  • Burns off adrenaline, releases muscle tension
  • Yoga stimulates the parasympathetic system
🔹 Sleep Hygiene:
  • Regular schedule, avoiding screens before bed, daytime physical activity
🔹 Positive Self-Statements:
  • "I am safe right now"
  • "I have tools to cope"
🧰 Safety Kit [14] — small items that calm and restore a sense of control:
  • Stress ball or item with pleasant texture
  • Favorite scent (essential oil, perfume)
  • Photo of loved ones or a safe place
  • Headphones with calming music
  • Water bottle (temperature grounding)

When to Seek Help

Red Flags:
  • Hypervigilance interferes with work, sleep, socializing
  • Inability to leave the house due to fear
  • Constant startling at every sound
  • Feeling of being watched
  • Thoughts of self-harm
  • Uncontrollable anger outbursts
