Caregiver Burnout and Prevention: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Caregiver Burnout Preventer" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainian caregivers caring for wounded soldiers, elderly relatives, and the ill during wartime.

Caring for wounded, ill, or elderly relatives is extremely exhausting work that often remains "invisible." Research shows that nearly 70% of caregivers report physical or emotional exhaustion, and over 40% experience depression [1]. Caregiver burnout is a state of physical, emotional, and mental exhaustion that can lead to serious health problems [2].

Main Causes of Burnout

  • Long hours of care without breaks
  • Lack of adequate support or backup help
  • High emotional load with limited outlets for emotions
  • Isolation from social and recreational life [3]
  • Financial burden and need to balance care with work

Signs of Burnout

Physical:

  • constant fatigue
  • sleep disturbances
  • appetite changes
  • frequent illnesses

Emotional:

  • irritability
  • anxiety
  • depression
  • feelings of hopelessness [4]

Cognitive:

  • forgetfulness
  • decision fatigue

Behavioral:

  • ignoring own needs
  • social isolation

Prevention Strategies

🔹 Prioritize Self-Care
Regular breaks, even short walks or alone time, can recharge energy [5]. It's important to maintain sleep routine, balanced nutrition, and attend your own medical checkups.
🔹 Set Boundaries
Learn to say "no" to additional responsibilities when overwhelmed, set realistic daily goals, and share responsibilities with others [6].
🔹 Ask for Help
Many caregivers try to do everything themselves, but experts emphasize the importance of delegating tasks [7]. Help can be specific: "Could you sit with Mom on Thursday so I can go to the doctor?"
🔹 Support Networks
Join support groups (online or local), talk with friends, therapist, or mentor [8]. Support groups help you realize you're not alone.
🔹 Evidence-Based Practices
Research shows the effectiveness of mindfulness breathing combined with music for reducing stress and burnout [9]. Mindfulness-Based Compassionate Living (MBCL) also reduces emotional exhaustion and depersonalization, increasing self-efficacy and self-compassion [10].
🔹 Respite Care
Temporary professional care that allows the caregiver to rest. This can be a few hours a week or a longer period [11].
🔹 Focus on Positives
The "three good things" technique (writing down three positive moments at the end of each day) helps increase happiness levels [12].

Ukrainian Context

In Ukraine, especially during wartime, caregivers are often left alone with problems. Initiatives like "Vseturbota" show how important support for caregivers is: social workers remind that "you are also human and have the right to your own life" [13]. Even short meetings with other caregivers can completely change mood and give energy.

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Thoughts of self-harm
  • Wishing for "this all to end"
  • Inability to get out of bed for weeks
  • Daily alcohol use to calm down
  • Fear of harming the care recipient
  • Daily panic attacks
