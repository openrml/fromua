Navigating Financial Crisis: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Financial Crisis Navigator" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainians experiencing financial crisis due to war — job loss, forced displacement, inflation, instability.

Financial crisis is not just about money, but also about stress, anxiety, and loss of control. Research shows that nearly 70% of people report significant financial stress, and it affects all areas of life [1]. During war, this stress intensifies due to job loss, forced displacement, and the need to help others.

Key Strategies for Surviving Financial Crisis

🔹 Stay Calm and Don't Panic
When we're frightened, we tend to make impulsive decisions that can cause more harm. "If you're feeling strong anxiety, don't move your money at all", advise financial therapists [2]. It's important to first "complete the stress cycle" (breathing, walk, talk with a friend), then make decisions [3].
🔹 Focus on What's Under Control
We can't control exchange rates, store prices, or employer decisions. But we can control our spending, savings, daily habits [4]. Listing what's within our power significantly reduces anxiety.
🔹 Prioritize Expenses ("Four Walls")
When income is unstable, prioritize basic needs first:
🍞 Food — essential groceries
🏠 Housing — rent/utilities
🚌 Transportation — for work/job seeking
💊 Medicine — medical needs
Everything else is secondary [5].
🔹 Build a Financial "Safety Cushion"
Even a small amount saved "just in case" gives a sense of control. Start small — $1-2 a week [6].
🔹 Restructure Debt
If you have loans, contact the bank and try to negotiate restructuring or payment holidays. Ignoring debt only worsens the situation [7].
🔹 Find Resources and Help
It's important to know where to find support: government programs, charitable funds, humanitarian aid, consultations with financial advisors [8].
🔹 Diversify Income
Even a small additional income (freelance, temporary work) can reduce anxiety and give a sense that you're not helpless [9].

Three-Layer Financial Security System

Abhishek Walia's concept proposes three layers of financial security [10]:

  1. Absolute Reserve: Cash available immediately (1-2 months of expenses)
  2. Predictable Expenses: Regular payments that can be planned
  3. Flexibility: Ability to adapt quickly — additional skills, social connections, community resources

Remember, the system matters more than the amount — even small steps in each layer create a sense of security.

Ukrainian Context

Government Support Programs:

  • eSupport (єПідтримка) — payments for IDPs, unemployment assistance
  • IDP Living Assistance — through the Diia portal
  • Utility Subsidies — for low-income households

Charitable Funds and Organizations:

  • Red Cross of Ukraine — humanitarian aid
  • Caritas Ukraine — support for vulnerable groups
  • "Come Back Alive" Foundation — support for military and their families
  • "Right to Protection" Charity Foundation — legal aid for IDPs

Useful Resources:

  • Lifeline Ukraine — crisis hotline: 7333
  • Free Legal Consultations — through the Free Legal Aid system

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Suicidal thoughts due to debt
  • Feeling that there's "no way out" of the financial situation
  • Lack of food due to lack of money
  • Fear of ending up on the street
  • Daily panic attacks about money
  • Willingness to do anything for money (risky offers)
