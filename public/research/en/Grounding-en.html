What is Grounding and How It Works: An Overview

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Grounding Instructor" role on fromua.life. Information has been adapted for the Ukrainian context.

What is Grounding?

Grounding is a set of coping strategies that help an individual shift their focus from internal anxious thoughts, panic, or dissociation to the external world and the present moment [1]. It is a way to return to the "here and now" by focusing on physical sensations or mental distractions [2].

Who Benefits from Grounding and When?

Grounding is effective for:

  • Panic attacks [3];
  • Acute anxiety and stress [4];
  • Dissociation (feelings of unreality, detachment from one's body) [5];
  • Flashbacks and intrusive memories in PTSD [6];
  • Emotional overwhelm and anger [7].

How It Works (The Science)?

When a person experiences anxiety or panic, the sympathetic nervous system (the "fight or flight" response) is activated, releasing adrenaline and cortisol [8]. Grounding helps activate the parasympathetic nervous system (the "rest and digest" response) through stimulation of the vagus nerve [9]. This leads to:

  • Decreased heart rate and blood pressure;
  • Reduced activity in the amygdala (the brain's fear center);
  • Shifting brain resources to the prefrontal cortex (responsible for rational thought) [10].

Main Types of Grounding Techniques

Research identifies three main categories [11]:

Sensory

Using the five senses to bring a person back into their body.

  • 5-4-3-2-1: Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you can smell, and 1 you can taste [12].
  • Temperature: Hold an ice cube, splash cold water on your face, take a hot shower.
  • Textures: Feel a smooth stone, soft fabric, or a pop-it toy.

Physical

Focusing on movement and sensations within the body.

  • Breathing: Square breathing (4-4-4-4) or the 4-7-8 technique [13].
  • Pressure: Press your heels firmly into the floor, hug yourself, use a weighted blanket.
  • Movement: Stretch, jump, walk, wiggle your toes.

Mental

Distracting the brain through cognitive tasks.

  • Counting: Count backward from 100, recite multiplication tables.
  • Categorizing: Name car brands, dog breeds, cities.
  • Describing: Describe a routine task in detail (e.g., how you brush your teeth).

Important Considerations

Grounding is not a replacement for therapy but a first-aid tool. For individuals with severe trauma, some techniques (especially breathing and physical ones) can sometimes trigger a reverse reaction—flashbacks or panic [14]. Therefore, it's crucial to offer choice and proceed gently.

Crisis indicators (thoughts of self-harm) require immediate contact with a professional [15].
