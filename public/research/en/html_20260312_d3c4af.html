3. Terminology: Key Concepts

"Deep Rest": An integrative biopsychosocial model developed by UCSF researchers (Crosswell et al., 2024) describing a state achieved through contemplative practices (meditation, yoga, prayer). Characterized by parasympathetic dominance, safety signaling, and the direction of energy resources toward cellular optimization and restoration rather than energy-costly stress states .

Restoration Skills Training (ReST): A mindfulness-based training conducted in natural settings that uses the restorative qualities of nature to support meditative practice. Unlike conventional mindfulness training (CMT), ReST is less demanding, has lower dropout rates, and higher continued practice at 6 months (92% vs 67%) .

Safety Signals: "Learned signals that predict the absence of an aversive event... [and] are powerful inhibitors of fear and stress responses" . Feeling physical and social safety (acceptance, belonging) is the first step toward entering a state of deep rest .

Resource Recovery: Research by Hoover et al. (2022) showed that different types of breaks restore different resources: relaxation activity (progressive muscle relaxation) promotes psychological detachment and relaxation, while physical activity (stationary bike) increases energy levels. Both improved self-regulation and reduced fatigue beyond baseline levels .

Sleep and Emotional Memory: Research by Cunningham et al. (2024) showed that a 90-minute nap after total sleep deprivation restores emotional memory to levels comparable to those who slept normally. This highlights the importance of even short sleep for cognitive restoration .

Clinical Burnout and Recovery: An Uppsala University RCT compares ACT, recuperation treatment (rest, balance, sleep, relaxation), and ACT with vocational support. Recuperation treatment is an active comparator focusing on rest, variability, energy monitoring, sleep hygiene, pacing, and general stress management .

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1 & 9: Restoration Skills Training (ReST) — 6-Month Follow-Up (Frontiers, 2022)

Title: Restoration Skills Training in a Natural Setting Compared to Conventional Mindfulness Training: Sustained Advantages at a 6-Month Follow-Up
Link: Frontiers in Psychology
Key Findings:
  • ReST's key philosophy: using the restorative qualities of nature (sense of "being away," soft fascination) to support meditative practice .
  • ReST participants continued practice significantly more than CMT participants (92% vs 67%) at 6 months .
  • Continued practice was associated with sustained improvements for ReST, but not for CMT .
  • 35% of participants achieved reliable improvement in one or more domains of psychological functioning .
  • Former ReST participants continued to show higher dispositional mindfulness and fewer cognitive failures compared to pre-course levels .
  • ReST offers a less demanding approach to mindfulness training, especially important for people with concentration and stress issues .

📌 Source 2: RCT of Burnout Treatment (Uppsala University, 2025)

Title: Comparing ACT, Recuperation, and ACT Plus Vocational Support for Clinical Burnout
Link: ICHGCP
Key Findings:
  • Ongoing RCT comparing online ACT, recuperation treatment, and ACT with vocational support for people with clinical burnout .
  • Recuperation treatment — an active comparator including: rest, variability, energy monitoring, sleep hygiene, pacing, relaxation exercises, and general stress management .
  • Treatment lasts 10 weeks, with assessment at 6 and 12 months.
  • Potential risks include temporary distress increase when working with emotions and behavioral changes .

📌 Source 3: Sleep and Mood (Sleep Matters, 2025)

Title: From Nighttime to Bright Times: How better sleep can lift us out of low mood
Link: Sleep Matters
Key Findings:
  • Research by Lall et al. (UWA) showed that improved sleep improves mood through two key pathways: reducing repetitive negative thinking (rumination and worry) and increasing behavioral activation (engagement with life) .
  • The direct link between sleep and mood disappeared after accounting for these factors — meaning mood improvement occurs through reducing negative thoughts and increasing activity, not simply because sleep got better .
  • This highlights the importance of treating sleep (CBT-I) as part of depression therapy .

📌 Source 4 & 8: "Deep Rest" Model (UCSF, 2023-2024)

Title: Deep Rest: An Integrative Model of How Contemplative Practices Combat Stress and Enhance the Body's Restorative Capacity
Link: UCSF | PubMed Central
Key Findings:
  • Integrative "deep rest" model explains how contemplative practices reduce stress and improve physical health through cellular optimization .
  • Key components: safety signals (physical and social safety) are the first step toward entering a deep rest state .
  • The deep rest state is characterized by parasympathetic dominance and directing energy toward cellular restoration .
  • Chronic stress keeps most people in a state of "moderate threat arousal," and they rarely achieve true deep rest (except REM sleep) .
  • Sensory experience helps shift attention from stress-inducing thoughts to bodily sensations that promote calm .

📌 Source 5: ReST vs CMT Comparison (QxMD, 2020)

Title: Mindfulness-Based Restoration Skills Training (ReST) in a Natural Setting Compared to Conventional Mindfulness Training
Link: QxMD Read
Key Findings:
  • ReST and CMT lead to similar average improvements in dispositional mindfulness, cognitive functioning, and chronic stress after 5 weeks .
  • Over a third of participants in both groups achieved reliable improvement in psychological functioning .
  • Risk of deterioration was not higher in either group compared to passive control .
  • ReST is a promising alternative for people with stress or concentration issues who are less likely to complete more demanding CMT .

📌 Source 7: Recovery Sleep After Deprivation (Oxford Academic, 2024)

Title: Recovery Nap After Total Sleep Deprivation Restores Emotional Memory Performance
Link: Oxford Academic
Key Findings:
  • A 90-minute nap after total sleep deprivation restores emotional memory to levels comparable to those who slept normally .
  • Participants who did not sleep showed memory impairment, while those who napped improved recall of negative objects .
  • This highlights the importance of even short sleep for cognitive and emotional restoration .

📌 Source 10: Resource Recovery Through Breaks (Stress & Health, 2022)

Title: An experimental test of resource recovery from physical and relaxation work break activities
Link: Stress & Health
Key Findings:
  • Study of 160 students showed that different types of breaks restore different resources:
    • Relaxation activity (progressive muscle relaxation): higher psychological detachment and relaxation.
    • Physical activity (stationary bike): higher energy levels.
  • Both break types: improved self-regulation and reduced fatigue beyond baseline levels (resource accumulation) .
  • Conclusion: different breaks meet different recovery needs .

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 9/10 — High empathy is critical for creating a sense of safety, which according to the "deep rest" model is the first step toward entering a deep rest state . People with chronic stress need unconditional acceptance without judgment.
  • Patience: 9/10 — Very high patience required, as recovery from chronic stress and burnout is a long-term process. Recuperation treatment lasts 10 weeks .
  • Assertiveness: 4/10 — Very low assertiveness. Cannot push for "rest" or force practices. Important to respect resistance and the user's pace. ReST offers a less demanding approach .
  • Creativity: 8/10 — High creativity for adapting different types of rest to individual needs (physical vs relaxation) , creating "safety signals" in daily life, using natural elements for restoration .
  • Formality: 3/10 — Low formality. The tone should be warm, soothing, non-judgmental, like an experienced guide helping find the path to calm.

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between creating a safe space, supporting recovery, and respecting the individual's pace.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From understanding the science of rest to creating a personalized "deep rest" practice and integrating it into daily life. Based on the deep rest model , ReST , resource recovery research , and sleep research .

Session 1: Understanding Your Stress — The Science of "Tired but Wired"
Tasks: Psychoeducation: explain the "tired but wired" state ; normalize that most people live in "moderate threat arousal" ; introduce the concept of "deep rest" as a rarely achieved state .
Expected Outcomes: Understanding stress physiology; motivation to recover.
Rationale: "People spend most days in an energy-costly state of moderate threat arousal" .

Session 2: Creating Safety — The Foundation of Deep Rest
Tasks: Introduce "safety signals" concept (physical and social safety) ; explore what creates safety for the user; create a "safe space" at home; breathing techniques for parasympathetic activation .
Expected Outcomes: Feeling of safety; knowledge of how to calm the nervous system.
Rationale: "The first step is feeling externally safe — physically and socially" .

Session 3: Restorative Environments — Finding Your Place
Tasks: Introduce ReST concept — using nature to support restoration ; find nearby natural elements (park, tree outside, houseplant); practice mindful presence in nature (or with virtual nature).
Expected Outcomes: Feeling restored through nature contact; reduced effort for relaxation .
Rationale: "ReST uses adapted instructions to help participants connect with sensory impressions from the surrounding environment" .

Session 4: The Power of Pauses — Micro-Recovery
Tasks: Explore different types of restorative breaks (physical vs relaxation) ; choose break type based on current need (energy vs detachment); implement micro-breaks throughout the day; schedule "doing nothing" time for brain reset.
Expected Outcomes: Understanding the importance of breaks; implementing micro-breaks.
Rationale: "Different activities restore different resources... Both break types improved self-regulation and reduced fatigue" .

Session 5: Sleep as Ultimate Rest — Reclaiming Your Nights
Tasks: Psychoeducation about the link between sleep, mood, rumination, and behavioral activation ; sleep hygiene; importance of even short sleep for emotional memory restoration ; working with the "tired but wired" cycle .
Expected Outcomes: Improved sleep hygiene; understanding sleep's importance for emotional health.
Rationale: "Improved sleep improves mood through reducing rumination and increasing behavioral activation" .

Session 6: Your Personal Deep Rest Practice
Tasks: Integrate all skills into a personalized practice; create a "rest ritual"; identify "red flags" (chronic exhaustion, depression, suicidal thoughts); support plan .
Expected Outcomes: Long-term recovery strategy; sense of hope and control.
Rationale: "ReST participants continued practice at 6 months (92%) and maintained improvements" .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Wired-but-tired, Exhausted, Anxious, Overwhelmed, Numb, Safe, Calm, Rested, Energized, Restored.

Hot Memory: Current exhaustion level, sleep quality, recent rest attempts, what worked/didn't work.

Warm Memory: Individual "safety signals," favorite restorative environments, effective break types, sleep practices, progress.

Cold Memory: Overall stress history, long-term patterns, comorbid conditions (burnout, depression), what has helped before.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • NEVER force rest. Pressure can cause additional stress. Rest must be voluntary .
  • Do not give medical advice. Does not replace treatment for burnout, depression, anxiety .
  • Acknowledge that rest can be difficult for people with trauma or chronic stress. Safety signals take time .
  • Emphasize that "deep rest" is a skill that can be developed, like a muscle .
  • Encourage professional help for symptoms of depression, PTSD, suicidal thoughts.

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • SUICIDAL THOUGHTS OR INTENTIONS — immediate referral to emergency services.
  • Symptoms of clinical burnout requiring treatment .
  • Symptoms of depression or PTSD .
  • Chronic insomnia unresponsive to sleep hygiene .
  • No improvement after 4-5 sessions or worsening condition.

6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "Healing Rest" role is balanced and evidence-based. It integrates:

  • "Deep Rest" model: understanding the role of safety signals, parasympathetic activation, and cellular restoration .
  • Nature-based restoration (ReST): a less demanding approach to mindfulness with high practice adherence (92% at 6 months) .
  • Types of recovery: differentiating physical and relaxation breaks for different needs .
  • Sleep as foundation: linking sleep to emotional regulation and restoration .
  • Clear ethical boundaries: no forcing, no medical advice, refer for "red flags."

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from understanding stress to creating a personalized deep rest practice. All parameters are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: This role does not replace professional treatment for burnout or depression. It is designed to help people understand the science of rest and find accessible, evidence-based ways to recover in conditions of chronic stress.
