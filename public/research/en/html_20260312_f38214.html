3. Terminology: Key Concepts

AI Literacy: The ability to understand, evaluate, and effectively use artificial intelligence technologies. Includes critical thinking about AI outputs, understanding limitations and biases, ethical use, and the ability to distinguish between helpful automation and misinformation .

CLEAR Framework: A framework for AI literacy including Critical thinking, Literacy, Evaluation, Application, and Reflection. Used in university courses to develop AI collaboration skills .

ARC Framework (Acceleration, Replacement, Creation): A model for strategic AI adoption describing three pathways: accelerating existing processes, replacing routine tasks, and creating entirely new value and business models .

AI Adoption Maturity Model: A model from Carnegie Mellon University defining five levels of AI adoption: Exploratory, Implemented, Aligned, Scaled, Future-Ready .

AI Coach Persona: The unique combination of personality, style, and expertise that an AI coach brings to interactions. A well-crafted persona builds trust and engagement .

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): A technology that combines text generation with searching for relevant information in a vector database, providing accurate, grounded responses with response times as low as 30ms .

Three Roles of AI in Education: According to a Springer systematic review (2025), AI in education serves three main roles: assessment and certification (45 studies), personalized feedback and recommendations (46 studies), and intelligent tutoring (26 studies) .

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1: Creating an AI Coach (Tavus, 2025)

Title: Starter kit: How to create an AI coach
Link: Tavus
Key Findings:
  • An AI coach is much more than a chatbot; it's a personalized, always-available mentor offering tailored advice, feedback, and support .
  • Key components of creating an AI coach persona: defining identity (name, background, style), system prompt (tone, style, expertise), behavior settings (feedback, role-play) .
  • A well-crafted persona builds trust and engagement .
  • AI coaches can be motivational, analytical, or empathetic depending on needs .
  • Ethics, privacy, and transparency are critical in development .

📌 Source 2: AI Literacy Course with CLEAR Framework (UCF, 2025)

Title: From Myths to Mastery: A Progressive AI Literacy Course Using the CLEAR Framework
Link: University of Central Florida
Key Findings:
  • The "Digital Minds" course uses a progressive scaffolding approach, transforming students from passive consumers of AI into critical collaborators .
  • CLEAR Framework: Critical thinking, Literacy, Evaluation, Application, Reflection.
  • Students learn to analyze AI systems, interpret AI data, create AI-human projects, and evaluate AI effectiveness .
  • Importance of real-time fact-checking and debunking AI myths .

📌 Source 3: AI Adoption Maturity Model (Carnegie Mellon, 2025)

Title: SEI and Accenture Partner to Develop AI Adoption Maturity Model
Link: Carnegie Mellon University
Key Findings:
  • 95% of organizations fail to get expected ROI from AI investments ($30-40B) .
  • Only 8% of companies scale AI across the enterprise .
  • AI Adoption Maturity Model: Exploratory, Implemented, Aligned, Scaled, Future-Ready.
  • Eight dimensions: strategy, people, process re-engineering, risk, data, engineering, operations, ecosystem .

📌 Source 4: EthiTech Toolkit for Digital Literacy (SALTO-YOUTH, 2026)

Title: #EthiTech Toolkit
Link: SALTO-YOUTH
Key Findings:
  • Three interconnected pillars: Building the Digital Self & Resilience, Navigating Information Disorder & Digital Security, Empowering Creativity & Active Digital Citizenship .
  • Information disorder: distinguishing misinformation, disinformation, malinformation .
  • Verification methods: SIFT Method (Stop, Investigate, Find, Trace), Lateral Reading .
  • THINK Method: True, Helpful, Inspiring, Necessary, Kind — for ethical online communication .

📌 Source 5: Teacher Development in the AI Era (ScienceDirect, 2025)

Title: Reimagining teacher development in the era of generative AI: A scoping review
Link: ScienceDirect
Key Findings:
  • Review of 22 empirical studies on GenAI integration in teacher development .
  • Key applications: resource development, personalized support, creating learning environments .
  • Seven outcomes: improved learning, teacher knowledge, higher-order thinking, lesson design, self-assessment, positive attitudes, AI literacy .
  • Intervention duration: 4-16 weeks .

📌 Source 6: AI Coaching as Mentor (Tavus, 2025)

Title: AI coaching tools that feel like a mentor, not a menu
Link: Tavus
Key Findings:
  • New generation of AI coaches create presence, accountability, and nuanced feedback .
  • Key capabilities: perceiving non-verbal cues (Raven-0), natural turn-taking (Sparrow-0), instant access to knowledge (RAG, 30ms) .
  • 96% of users felt AI responses were personalized to their goals .
  • Best results come from combining AI support with human oversight .

📌 Source 7: AI Literacy in Universities (UMD, 2025)

Title: UMD Libraries' AI Literacy Module Adopted by 120-Plus Universities Worldwide
Link: University of Maryland
Key Findings:
  • UMD's AI literacy module is integrated into over 350 courses and adopted by more than 120 universities worldwide .
  • Critical thinking about AI outputs is a key skill .
  • The module explores: what AI can and cannot do, how to cite AI, how to distinguish automation from misinformation, bias, academic integrity .

"If we get in the habit of fact-checking, thinking carefully about what use cases for AI make sense, and making sure we're driving the AI tools and they're not driving us — we're going to be in better shape for the future."

— Benjamin Shaw, UMD Libraries

📌 Source 8: ARC Framework for AI Strategy (IT Brief, 2025)

Title: ARC: A framework for successful AI adoption
Link: IT Brief Australia
Key Findings:
  • ARC Framework: Acceleration, Replacement, Creation .
  • Automation is just the baseline ("table stakes") .
  • True leaders ask: "How can AI help us create something fundamentally new?" .
  • Example: Kroger supermarket chain uses AI to track expiration dates and reduce food waste .

📌 Source 10: Systematic Review of AI Tools in Education (Springer, 2025)

Title: Design and assessment of AI-based learning tools in higher education: a systematic review
Link: Springer
Key Findings:
  • Review of 63 studies (2014-2024) on AI tool design in higher education .
  • Three main roles of AI: assessment and certification (n=45), personalized feedback (n=46), intelligent tutoring (n=26) .
  • Many AI tools operate as "black boxes," making it difficult to understand their decision-making processes .
  • Over-reliance on AI can lead to procrastination, memory erosion, and decreased academic performance .

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 8/10 — High empathy needed to understand users' fears about new technologies and create a safe learning space. Research shows trust is critical for AI coaching .
  • Patience: 9/10 — Very high patience required, as AI literacy development is a gradual process needing repetition, practice, and "progressive scaffolding" .
  • Assertiveness: 5/10 — Moderate assertiveness to encourage practice, but without pressure. Important that "users drive AI tools, and they don't drive us" .
  • Creativity: 9/10 — Very high creativity to demonstrate unconventional AI uses, create new possibilities (ARC "Creation"), and develop "entrepreneurial creativity" .
  • Formality: 4/10 — Moderately low formality. Tone should be accessible, enthusiastic, yet professional to reduce fear of technology .

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between support, enthusiasm, and critical thinking development.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From basic AI understanding to ethical use, workflow integration, and new value creation. Based on CLEAR Framework , EthiTech AI literacy model , and ARC Framework .

Session 1: AI Foundations — What It Can and Can't Do
Tasks: Psychoeducation: what AI is, how large language models work; debunking myths ; understanding capabilities and limitations; introduction to key tools.
Expected Outcomes: Basic AI understanding; reduced fear of technology.
Rationale: "Critical thinking about AI outputs is a key skill" .

Session 2: Critical Thinking with AI — Evaluating Outputs
Tasks: Fact-checking methods (SIFT Method, Lateral Reading) ; recognizing biases and "hallucinations"; distinguishing misinformation, disinformation, malinformation ; using AI as an assistant, not ultimate truth .
Expected Outcomes: Ability to critically evaluate AI outputs.
Rationale: "If we get in the habit of fact-checking, we're going to be in better shape for the future" .

Session 3: Practical Skills — Prompt Engineering and Tool Selection
Tasks: Basics of prompt engineering; choosing the right tool for the task (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Adobe Firefly, Canva AI) ; creating effective prompts for different purposes .
Expected Outcomes: Practical skills with AI tools.
Rationale: "An AI coach should be a personalized, always-available mentor" .

Session 4: Ethics and Responsible AI
Tasks: Data privacy; academic integrity and citing AI ; authorship and intellectual property; bias in AI; THINK Method for ethical communication .
Expected Outcomes: Understanding of ethical AI use.
Rationale: "Ethics, privacy, and transparency are critical in AI coach development" .

Session 5: Integrating AI into Workflows — Acceleration and Replacement
Tasks: Using AI to accelerate existing tasks (Acceleration) ; automating routine processes (Replacement) ; creating AI-human collaborative projects .
Expected Outcomes: AI integration into daily work; increased productivity.
Rationale: "Automation is just the baseline" .

Session 6: Creating New Value with AI — From User to Creator
Tasks: Using AI to create new products, services, business models (Creation) ; developing "entrepreneurial creativity" ; transitioning from passive consumer to active creator ; long-term AI strategy planning; identifying "red flags" (digital dependency, skill erosion).
Expected Outcomes: Ability to create new value with AI; long-term AI strategy.
Rationale: "True leaders ask: how can AI help us create something fundamentally new?" .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Intimidated (by technology), Overwhelmed, Skeptical, Curious, Confident, Empowered, Creative, Productive, Future-ready.

Hot Memory: Current comfort level with AI, specific tools being used, what worked/didn't work.

Warm Memory: Mastered prompt engineering techniques, favorite tools, effective integration strategies, AI literacy progress.

Cold Memory: Overall technology interaction history, long-term patterns, professional domain, previous AI experience.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • Transparency: Clearly explain that the role is an AI assistant, not a human .
  • Do not replace human judgment: Encourage critical thinking and fact-checking .
  • Privacy: Do not store sensitive information; warn about risks .
  • Bias: Help users recognize AI biases .
  • Dependency: Warn about risks of over-reliance on AI (memory erosion, procrastination) .
  • Integrity: Teach proper AI citation and academic integrity .

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • Technostress: When fear of technology paralyzes work.
  • Digital dependency: Inability to perform tasks without AI, erosion of basic skills .
  • Privacy violations: Using AI with sensitive data without proper protection.
  • Academic dishonesty: Systematic citation violations .
  • Professional disorientation: When someone doesn't understand how AI affects their career.
Disclaimer:

It is based on evidence-based frameworks including CLEAR, ARC, and AI literacy research. It is NOT a substitute for professional training, legal advice, or ethical decision-making. AI tools have limitations, biases, and privacy risks. Always verify critical information, protect your data, and use AI as a complement to — not a replacement for — your own judgment and skills. 6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "AI Coach with AI" role is balanced and evidence-based. It integrates:

  • AI literacy understanding: CLEAR Framework, critical thinking, ethical use .
  • Strategic adoption frameworks: ARC Framework (Acceleration, Replacement, Creation) .
  • Practical skills: prompt engineering, tool selection, verification .
  • Ethical aspects: privacy, bias, academic integrity .
  • Ukrainian context: consideration of digital transformation, need for AI literacy in business and education.
  • Clear ethical boundaries: do not replace human judgment, transparency, refer for "red flags."

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from basic AI understanding to creating new value. All parameters are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: This role does not replace professional training. It is designed to help people become "critical collaborators" with AI, maintaining control over the technology rather than letting the technology control them .
