Triggers and Flashbacks: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Triggers & Flashbacks Navigator" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainians experiencing war trauma — veterans, civilians in combat zones, people who have lost loved ones or their homes.

Flashbacks are sudden, vivid, involuntary memories of a traumatic event that create the feeling that the trauma is happening again here and now [1].

Types of Flashbacks

  • Visual — seeing images
  • Bodily — feeling pain, tension, smells
  • Emotional — sudden experience of fear, horror, shame
  • Auditory — sounds, voices

Triggers are stimuli that activate flashbacks: sounds, smells, places, dates, words, situations that remind of the trauma [2].

Key Strategies for Working with Triggers and Flashbacks

🔹 Grounding — returning to the present moment through the senses. 5-4-3-2-1 technique (see, feel, hear, smell, taste) [3].
🔹 Distinguishing "Then" and "Now" — reminding yourself where you are now, what year it is, what is safe [4].
🔹 Creating a "Safe Place" — internal or real space where you can retreat [5].
🔹 Trigger Map — identifying individual triggers, their intensity, and response strategies [6].
🔹 Body Work — flashbacks are stored in the body, so relaxation techniques are important [7].
🔹 Planning Difficult Situations — preparing for days, places, events that may be triggering.
🔹 Self-Compassion — flashbacks don't mean regression, they are part of healing [8].

Detailed Techniques

🧘 5-4-3-2-1 Technique (eyes remain open):
  • 5 things you can see around you
  • 4 things you can touch
  • 3 sounds you can hear
  • 2 smells you can notice
  • 1 taste you can sense
🧘 Distinguishing "Then and Now":
  • Name today's date
  • Name where you are right now
  • Name 3 safe objects in the room
  • Say to yourself: "It's [year]. I am safe. What I'm experiencing is a memory"
🧘 Trigger Map — a self-completion table:
  • Trigger: what activates it?
  • Situation: where/when does it usually happen?
  • Intensity (1-10): how strongly do you react?
  • Strategy: what helps you cope?
🧘 Physical Grounding:
  • Feel your feet on the floor, press them down
  • Feel your hands on your thighs, press gently
  • Hold something cold or warm in your hands
  • Stretch, do a few simple movements
🧘 Safe Place (Visualization):
  • Imagine a place where you feel completely safe
  • Add details: colors, sounds, smells, textures
  • Imagine yourself in this place, breathe calmly
  • You can "visit" this place after a flashback
🧘 Flashback Action Plan:
  1. Stop — acknowledge this is a flashback
  2. Ground — 5-4-3-2-1
  3. Distinguish — name date, place, safe objects
  4. Calm your body — breathing, physical grounding
  5. Restore strength — after a flashback, you need rest

Ukrainian Context

For Ukrainians experiencing war trauma, important resources include:

  • Lifeline Ukraine — 24/7 crisis hotline: 7333 (free from mobile)
  • Veteran Hub — psychological support for veterans and their families
  • Ministry of Health Psychological Support — mental health hotline
  • Veteran Support Groups — in veteran spaces across Ukraine

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Flashbacks don't stop or become more frequent
  • Fear of sleeping due to nightmares
  • Thoughts of self-harm to "make it stop"
  • Feeling like you're losing your mind
  • Inability to leave the house due to fear of triggers
  • Daily thoughts of death
