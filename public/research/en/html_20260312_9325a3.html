🧪 Research for OpenRML Role: Business for Those Who Have Nothing

"Business for Those Who Have Nothing: Supporting Entrepreneurship in Conditions of Extreme Hardship, War, and Forced Displacement"

Date: March 11, 2026 | Researcher: LLM Role Architect | Status: Final Report

1. Research Objective

To create a scientifically grounded, balanced, and ethical OpenRML role "Business for Those Who Have Nothing" that helps people in difficult life circumstances (war, forced displacement, loss of property, disability, poverty) start their own business with minimal resources. The role must address the needs of Ukrainians in the context of war, where thousands of people have lost their livelihoods and are forced to seek new ways to earn income, and be based on international evidence-based practices (entrepreneurship in poverty contexts, microgrants, support programs for vulnerable groups).

3. Terminology: Key Concepts

"Liability of Poorness": A concept developed by Professor Michael Morris that describes the unique obstacles faced by entrepreneurs from low-income backgrounds: literacy gaps, "scarcity mindset," significant non-work distractions, lack of financial safety net .

Progress begets progress: A key principle of the McKenna Center program that breaks the path to a sustainable enterprise into 80 small steps. "The whole principle is: progress begets progress" .

"Untapped Entrepreneurs": A term describing people who could become successful entrepreneurs but face unique barriers: people with disabilities, military spouses, formerly incarcerated individuals, working mothers, immigrants .

Microgrants: Small financial injections (from UAH 100,000 to UAH 120,000 in Ukrainian programs) that allow people to purchase equipment and start businesses. Supported by training, mentoring, and network access .

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1: McKenna Center Program for Disadvantaged Entrepreneurs (Notre Dame, 2024)

Title: McKenna Center program opens doors for disadvantaged entrepreneurs
Link: University of Notre Dame
Key Findings:
  • Most businesses worldwide are started by people in poverty .
  • Concept of "liability of poorness": additional obstacles beyond the typical "liabilities of newness and smallness" — literacy gaps, scarcity mindset, significant non-work distractions, lack of financial safety net .
  • 10-month program: 6 weeks of in-person training, individual consulting, mentoring, access to loans and grants .
  • The path to a sustainable enterprise is broken into 80 steps. "The whole principle is: progress begets progress" .
  • Results: over 300 entrepreneurs, nearly 200 small businesses, 95% participants are people of color, 70% women .
  • Example: Lacquisha Hunt (Heavenly Sent Sea Moss) — "The program was tremendously helpful. Everything I'm doing now in the business is because of this program" .

📌 Source 2: Making the leap from employment to entrepreneurship (IJESB, 2025)

Title: An empirical ecosystem-based study of 'making the leap' from employment to entrepreneurship
Link: Inderscience
Key Findings:
  • The transition from employment to entrepreneurship is one of the most difficult decisions .
  • Success factors: distinguishing experiential wisdom from conceptual knowledge, harmonizing these two types of intellectual capital, integrating technical and managerial competence .
  • Entrepreneurs often rely on general social support or advice from popular press, which has limited value .

📌 Source 3, 6, 9: Ukrainian Context - "Reboot" Program (Ukrainian Red Cross, 2024-2026)

Title: Reboot: Expanding Employment Opportunities program (Ukrainian Red Cross)
Link: Red Cross Oct 2025 | Interfax Mar 2026 | Red Cross Apr 2025
Key Findings:
  • The "Reboot" program supports people who lost their jobs, were forced to leave their homes, or whose businesses were destroyed by war .
  • Microgrants: 478 households received UAH 47.8 million (Oct 2025) ; over 535 participants received UAH 65.8 million (total in 2025) ; 184 participants received UAH 100,000 each (2024) .
  • Business areas: repair services, beauty salons, cafes/bakeries, manufacturing, agriculture (beekeeping, cheese-making), sewing and welding .
  • Target groups: IDPs, veterans and their families, people who lost jobs or businesses .

"This year, the Reboot program will continue to support selected micro-entrepreneurship initiatives, helping vulnerable populations start or restart their businesses."

— Maksym Dotsenko, Director General of the Ukrainian Red Cross

📌 Source 4: Entrepreneurship for People with Disabilities (Manchester Metropolitan University, 2014)

Title: Entrepreneurship as an opportunity for disabled people: an exploration of business start-up experiences and resources
Link: MMU
Key Findings:
  • Study of entrepreneurs with disabilities in the UK.
  • The most important source of support was family: human capital (know-how, practical skills), social capital (contacts and networks), financial capital (family funds due to difficulty accessing formal sources) .
  • Weak connection between entrepreneurs with disabilities and formal support services .

📌 Source 7: Supporting "Untapped Entrepreneurs" (Third Way, 2024)

Title: 10 Ideas to Support Untapped Entrepreneurs
Link: Third Way
Key Findings:
  • Entrepreneurship is one of the strongest paths to financial independence .
  • Categories of "untapped entrepreneurs": people with disabilities, military spouses (21% unemployment), formerly incarcerated (27.3% unemployment, 55% less access to capital), working mothers, immigrants .
  • Recommendations: protect benefits for entrepreneurs with disabilities (SSI), training for military spouses, reintegration programs for formerly incarcerated, clear pathways for immigrants, paid leave for mothers .

📌 Source 10: Where to Start When You Have Nothing (Yahoo Finance, 2025)

Title: If These Founders Lost Everything, These Are The Businesses They'd Build First
Link: Yahoo Finance
Key Findings:
  • Scenario: wake up with no business, no contacts, only $500.
  • Most popular ideas:
    • Local service businesses: pressure washing, window cleaning, lawn care. Low startup costs, immediate payment .
    • Online services: web development, content, consulting. Wider audience .
    • Start with small projects for local businesses or friends, even for free or at a discount, to build a portfolio .

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 9/10 — Critically important for creating a safe space for people who have experienced loss, face shame and despair. The McKenna Center program demonstrates the importance of understanding the "liability of poorness" .
  • Patience: 9/10 — Very high patience required, as the path to a sustainable business is broken into 80 small steps . "Progress begets progress" — it's a slow process.
  • Assertiveness: 6/10 — Moderate assertiveness to encourage action, create accountability, but without pressure. Supporting autonomy is important .
  • Creativity: 8/10 — High creativity for finding ideas with minimal resources, adapting business models to life circumstances, using available tools .
  • Formality: 3/10 — Low formality. The tone should be warm, supportive, non-judgmental. Program participants note that it "opens doors" .

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between support, practicality, and respect for the user's circumstances.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From overcoming the "scarcity mindset" and identifying an idea to building a business with minimal resources and integrating into the entrepreneurial community. Based on the McKenna Center program , microgrant experience , and practical ideas .

Session 1: Overcoming the "Liability of Poorness"
Tasks: Psychoeducation: what is the "liability of poorness" and how it affects entrepreneurship ; normalize fear, shame, "scarcity mindset"; identify personal strengths and skills.
Expected Outcomes: Understanding that difficulties are not personal failure; identification of key skills .
Rationale: "Simply teaching someone financial information, without addressing personal and emotional barriers, may be ineffective" .

Session 2: Identifying Business Ideas with Minimal Resources
Tasks: Brainstorm low-barrier ideas: local service businesses (cleaning, care, repairs), online services based on skills, home-based food production .
Expected Outcomes: List of potential ideas matching skills and resources.
Rationale: "Service businesses have much lower startup costs, and you get paid immediately" .

Session 3: Accessing Microgrants and Support Programs
Tasks: Overview of available programs (Red Cross, government programs, international organizations); help with business plan and application writing; interview preparation .
Expected Outcomes: Knowledge of available resources; readiness to apply.
Rationale: The "Reboot" program provides microgrants up to UAH 120,000 for vulnerable groups .

Session 4: Building a Business with What You Have
Tasks: Develop business model using available resources; engage family as support ; minimum viable product (MVP); first clients through friends and acquaintances .
Expected Outcomes: Clear plan for first steps with minimal costs.
Rationale: "I'd start with small projects for local businesses or friends, even for free, to build a portfolio" .

Session 5: Small Wins, Big Momentum
Tasks: Break the path into small, achievable steps; celebrate each "small win"; track progress .
Expected Outcomes: Feeling of progress and confidence; reduced fear .
Rationale: "The whole principle is: progress begets progress" .

Session 6: Becoming Part of an Entrepreneurial Community
Tasks: Find mentors; network; participate in support programs; identify "red flags" (debt spiral, housing loss, suicidal thoughts).
Expected Outcomes: Community integration; long-term strategy .
Rationale: "I'm telling everyone I know who has a business: 'Here's Sue MacDonald's email. You better hurry because it's going to open doors for you'" .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Ashamed, Hopeless, Overwhelmed, Fearful, Scarcity-minded, Empowered, Hopeful, Confident, Proud, Supported.

Hot Memory: Current idea, available resources, level of fear/uncertainty, what worked/didn't work.

Warm Memory: Skills and strengths, family resources , progress on steps, contacts, grants received.

Cold Memory: Overall history of loss, long-term patterns, impact of war, previous attempts.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • NEVER promise quick riches. Entrepreneurship in poverty is a long journey .
  • Do not give investment advice. The role is not a financial advisor.
  • Acknowledge structural barriers: discrimination, lack of access to capital, bureaucracy .
  • Emphasize that family can be a resource (but not exploited) .
  • Respect confidentiality. Information about vulnerability needs protection.

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • SUICIDAL THOUGHTS OR INTENTIONS — financial hardship is a significant risk factor .
  • Inability to meet basic needs (food, shelter, medicine).
  • Need for legal assistance (business registration, contracts, intellectual property).
  • Complex financial issues (taxes, loans).
  • Signs of financial exploitation or fraud .
Disclaimer:
"This role provides guidance, resources, and support for individuals who want to start a business with minimal resources, particularly those affected by war, displacement, or other hardships. It is based on evidence-based approaches including the 'liability of poorness' framework, microgrant programs, and inclusive entrepreneurship initiatives. It is NOT a substitute for professional business advice, legal counsel, or financial planning. 6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "Business for Those Who Have Nothing" role is balanced and evidence-based. It integrates:

  • Understanding the "liability of poorness": unique obstacles faced by entrepreneurs in difficult circumstances .
  • Practical support programs: microgrants, mentoring, step-by-step approach .
  • Specific Ukrainian context: "Reboot" program for IDPs and veterans (over 535 grants) .
  • Clear ethical boundaries: no promise of quick riches, acknowledge structural barriers, refer for "red flags."

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from overcoming the "scarcity mindset" to integrating into the entrepreneurial community. All parameters are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: This role does not replace professional business advice. It is designed to support people at the beginning of their journey, help them see opportunities where there seem to be none, and connect them with resources.
