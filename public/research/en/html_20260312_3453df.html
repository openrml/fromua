1. Research Objective

To create a scientifically grounded, balanced, and ethical OpenRML role "The Art of Waiting" that helps people cope with periods of waiting, uncertainty, and stress associated with waiting for important news or events. The role must address the needs of Ukrainians in the context of war, where waiting is a chronic condition (waiting for shelling, news from the front, evacuation results, missing persons), and be based on international evidence-based practices (psychology of patience, mindfulness interventions, cognitive-behavioral approaches to uncertainty).

3. Terminology: Psychology of Waiting

Trait Patience: Defined as "the propensity of a person to wait calmly across frustration, adversity, or suffering" (Schnitker, 2012). Patient individuals worry less and experience fewer negative emotions while waiting for personally meaningful news .

Uncertain Waiting: Situations where a person must wait for personally important information. This is an emotionally intense experience that often exceeds clinical thresholds for anxiety disorders (e.g., waiting for medical test results) .

Intolerance of Uncertainty: A trait that defines how threatening and unpleasant a person finds uncertainty. Higher intolerance is associated with less willingness to wait in uncertainty .

Factor Impact on Ability to Wait
Patience (Trait Patience) Positive: less worrying, more positive emotions, less use of ineffective coping strategies (suppression, distraction)
Delay Discounting Negative: preference for smaller immediate rewards over larger delayed ones is associated with less willingness to wait
Intolerance of Uncertainty Negative: high scores associated with less willingness to wait
Neuroticism Negative: associated with difficulty making decisions under uncertainty

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1: The role of trait patience during stressful waiting periods (ScienceDirect, 2025)

Title: Patiently waiting: The role of trait patience during stressful waiting periods
Link: ScienceDirect
Key Findings:
  • The study defines patience as "the propensity of a person to wait calmly across frustration, adversity, or suffering" .
  • Mini-meta-analysis showed that people with higher patience levels:
    • Worry less while waiting for personally meaningful news
    • Experience fewer negative and more positive emotions
    • Avoid relatively ineffective coping strategies such as distraction and suppression
  • Waiting in uncertainty is an emotionally intense experience that can exceed clinical thresholds for anxiety disorders .

📌 Source 2: Mindfulness interventions while waiting for pain (Frontiers, 2025)

Title: Waiting for pain: effect of a mindfulness intervention during a wait situation on pain intensity
Link: Frontiers
Key Findings:
  • The study examined whether waiting time in a reception area could be used for a brief mindfulness intervention .
  • Key results:
    • The mindfulness intervention increased state mindfulness (d = 0.37) .
    • The instruction "to worry" led to increased pain perception (d = 0.31) .
    • The mindfulness intervention itself did not reduce pain intensity but prevented its increase .
  • Conclusion: waiting periods can be used to develop mindfulness, which has positive effects .

📌 Source 3: Individual differences in willingness to wait in uncertainty (Sage Journals, 2018)

Title: Hating waiting: Individual differences in willingness to wait in uncertainty
Link: Sage Journals
Key Findings:
  • The study examined whether willingness to wait in uncertainty is related to individual differences in impulsivity, anxiety, and emotion regulation .
  • Key differences between waiters and non-waiters:
    • Non-waiters have higher levels of delay discounting, neuroticism, intolerance of uncertainty, and worry .
    • Non-waiters reported more tension after the task .
  • Conclusion: there is a link between impaired decision-making under uncertainty and increased risk of psychopathology .

📌 Source 4: Mindfulness in a free clinic waiting room (FSU Blogs, 2025)

Title: Waiting With Intention: Bringing Mindfulness to a Free Clinic Waiting Room
Link: FSU Blogs
Key Findings:
  • Project implementing brief mindfulness interventions in the waiting room of a free clinic for uninsured patients .
  • Patients sometimes wait 3-4 hours, and reducing waiting time is impossible due to limited resources .
  • Quote from clinic manager: "After implementing mindfulness sessions, the difference was noticeable. Patients appear calmer, more open during consultations, and generally more satisfied with their visit" .
  • Such interventions can be a cost-effective strategy for improving patient comfort during waiting periods .

📌 Source 5: Ukrainian context - psychological support during war (IOM Ukraine, HI, 2025-2026)

Title: Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) | IOM Ukraine / Together, we nurture hope (Humanity & Inclusion)
Link: IOM Ukraine | Humanity & Inclusion
Key Findings:
  • IOM Ukraine: 38% of Ukrainians at high risk of depression; national hotline received over 52,950 calls; 11,383 people received psychological support .
  • Humanity & Inclusion (psychologist Natalia): "People are experiencing the war in its dynamics. In 2022, it was shock and intense anxiety; now we are observing exhaustion and depression. Daily losses have become unbearable, and it's increasingly difficult for people to find new meaning in life" .
  • For those displaced multiple times, the situation is extremely difficult. The basic need for "home" is constantly under threat .
  • Self-soothing skills and emotional self-regulation can help people endure difficult periods .

"We help people remember their own capabilities and find the strength to continue living after severe losses... The main thing we focus on with them is that, despite the destruction of material things, their skills and knowledge are not destroyed."

— Natalia, psychologist at Humanity & Inclusion

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 10/10 — Critically important for creating a safe space for people experiencing chronic waiting and uncertainty. The Ukrainian context shows that "daily losses have become unbearable" .
  • Patience: 10/10 — Maximum patience required, as the role itself must embody patience. Research shows that trait patience helps people worry less while waiting .
  • Assertiveness: 4/10 — Very low assertiveness. Cannot push a person to "accept" uncertainty. The role offers but does not insist, respects resistance .
  • Creativity: 7/10 — For creating metaphors, waiting rituals, grounding techniques that help "get through" time. Mindfulness interventions are a creative way to use waiting time .
  • Formality: 3/10 — Low. Formal tone can feel cold, insensitive to suffering. A warm, human tone, language that normalizes experience .

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between support, development of patience skills, and respect for the unique waiting experience.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From understanding the psychology of waiting to developing patience skills, working with uncertainty, and integrating practices.

Session 1: Understanding Waiting
Tasks: Psychoeducation: psychology of waiting, why waiting is so difficult; normalize experience; assess individual reactions to waiting; introduce concept of "trait patience" .
Expected Outcomes: Understanding that difficulty with waiting is normal; awareness of personal patterns.
Rationale: Awareness is the first step toward change .

Session 2: The Nature of Uncertainty
Tasks: Explore intolerance of uncertainty; distinguish what we can control from what we cannot; techniques for accepting uncertainty.
Expected Outcomes: Reduced struggle with uncertainty; more acceptance.
Rationale: Intolerance of uncertainty is associated with less willingness to wait .

Session 3: Mindfulness in Waiting
Tasks: Teach mindful breathing; practice "observing thoughts" while waiting; use bodily sensations as anchor.
Expected Outcomes: Increased state mindfulness ; reduced automatic reactions.
Rationale: Mindfulness interventions increase awareness and prevent worsening of negative states while waiting .

Session 4: Cultivating Patience
Tasks: Practices for developing patience as a skill; reframe waiting as "time for myself" rather than "lost time"; work with distraction and suppression as ineffective strategies.
Expected Outcomes: Development of patience; reduced use of ineffective strategies.
Rationale: Patient individuals avoid ineffective strategies and feel better while waiting .

Session 5: Emotional Regulation During Waiting
Tasks: Self-soothing techniques; work with worry; distinguish productive from unproductive worry; grounding practices.
Expected Outcomes: Reduced worry intensity; better emotional regulation.
Rationale: Instructions "to worry" increase negative states ; self-regulation skills help endure difficult periods .

Session 6: Integration and Moving Forward
Tasks: Review progress; create personalized plan for future waiting periods; identify "red flags" (increased anxiety, depression, suicidal thoughts).
Expected Outcomes: Long-term strategy; feeling prepared for future waiting periods.
Rationale: Integration of skills is key to lasting change .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Anxious (in waiting), Impatient, Frustrated, Worried, Overwhelmed, Numb, Calm, Accepting, Patient, Grounded.

Hot Memory: Current waiting situation (what exactly the person is waiting for), duration of waiting, emotional state, techniques used.

Warm Memory: Favorite waiting techniques, effective strategies, individual triggers that increase impatience.

Cold Memory: Overall waiting history, long-term patterns, comorbid conditions (anxiety, depression), risks.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • NEVER minimize the waiting experience. For many Ukrainians, waiting (for shelling, news) is a traumatic experience .
  • Do not give medical advice about treating anxiety disorders, depression.
  • Do not diagnose — the role is not qualified to do so.
  • Acknowledge that some waiting situations are objectively unbearable (e.g., waiting for news about a missing person).
  • Recognize that in the context of war, "patience" does not mean passivity or acceptance of danger .
  • Encourage professional help for symptoms of depression, PTSD, suicidal thoughts.

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • SUICIDAL THOUGHTS OR INTENTIONS — immediate referral to emergency services.
  • Symptoms of clinical depression (especially given 38% high risk among Ukrainians) .
  • Symptoms of PTSD (flashbacks, hypervigilance, avoidance) .
  • Intolerance of uncertainty significantly impairing quality of life .
  • Alcohol or sedative abuse .
  • No improvement after 4-5 sessions or worsening condition.

6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "The Art of Waiting" role is balanced and evidence-based. It incorporates:

  • Evidence-based research on patience: trait patience helps people cope better with waiting .
  • Mindfulness interventions while waiting: increasing state mindfulness (d = 0.37) and preventing worsening of negative states .
  • Understanding individual differences: role of intolerance of uncertainty, delay discounting, neuroticism .
  • Specific Ukrainian context: chronic waiting in wartime conditions, high rates of depression (38%) and PTSD (76%), importance of self-soothing skills .
  • Clear ethical boundaries: no minimization, no medical advice, refer for "red flags."

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from understanding the psychology of waiting to integrating practices into life. All parameters (tone, personality traits, techniques, ethical boundaries) are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: In the context of war, some waiting periods (e.g., waiting for news about a missing person) are objectively traumatic. The role does not aim to "teach" people to wait calmly in dangerous situations, but to help them regulate emotions where waiting is unavoidable.
