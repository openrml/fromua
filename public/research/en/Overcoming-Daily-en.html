Overcoming Daily Chaos and Restoring Control: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Daily Life Reset Companion" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainians living in wartime conditions — limited space (shelters, temporary housing), energy constraints, the need to quickly gather belongings during alarms, constant stress.

When life feels chaotic, the simplest daily tasks — preparing food, cleaning a room, planning the day — can become an overwhelming burden. Research confirms that organizing space and creating simple routines directly impact mental health, lower cortisol levels, and provide a sense of control [1].

Key Strategies for Overcoming Daily Chaos

🔹 Small Steps Instead of Deep Cleaning
Even 15 minutes a day, split into three 5-minute sessions (morning planning, midday reset, evening preparation), can create amazing clarity and order without exhaustion [2]. Short, regular efforts work better than infrequent "deep cleans."
🔹 One-Touch Rule
This method involves touching an item only once between using it and putting it away. Instead of placing a jacket on a chair and hanging it later, you hang it immediately. This minimizes procrastination and prevents clutter buildup [3].
🧹 Mount Vernon Method
A structured approach to cleaning: start in one corner of the room and move clockwise, completing one task at a time. This minimizes decision fatigue and creates a sense of progress with each step [4].
🔹 Creating a "Menu" Instead of a To-Do List
Instead of a long list of obligatory tasks, create a "menu" of pleasant and useful activities (walking, reading, cooking) from which you can choose depending on mood and energy [5].
🔹 Five-Minute Resets
When the day spirals out of control, five mindful minutes are enough to lower stress hormone levels, improve focus, and make better decisions [6]. This could be deep breathing, a short stretch, or a screen-free pause.
🔹 Creating Drop Zones
Organize small spaces (baskets, trays) near the entrance, at your desk, where items can be quickly placed without cluttering surfaces [7]. This significantly eases maintaining order.
🔹 The Connection Between Space and Psyche
Clean surfaces contribute to a calmer, more organized environment, and cleaning becomes faster and more efficient [8]. When information and things have their place, the constant tension of searching and decision-making disappears.

Ukrainian Context: Adapting to Wartime Conditions

How to Apply These Strategies in Extreme Conditions:

  • In shelters and temporary housing: create a "go-bag" with essentials, organize a small "comfort zone" even in a basement
  • With limited energy: use 5-minute "energy snacks" instead of exhausting cleaning sessions
  • During frequent air raids: have a separate "alarm container" for documents, medicines, essentials
  • During power outages: plan tasks for daylight hours, use a morning ritual to create structure

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Inability to get out of bed for weeks, with home in complete chaos
  • Lack of food due to inability to prepare it because of kitchen clutter
  • Fear that someone will come in and see how you live
  • Suicidal thoughts due to hopelessness
  • Panic attacks when trying to clean
