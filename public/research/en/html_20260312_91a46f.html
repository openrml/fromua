Date: March 11, 2026 | Researcher: LLM Role Architect | Status: Final Report

1. Research Objective

To create a scientifically grounded, balanced, and ethical OpenRML role "The Community Builder" that helps leaders, activists, and organizations develop and strengthen communities. The role must address the needs of Ukrainians in the context of war, where communities have become the foundation of resilience, volunteerism, and mutual aid, and be based on international evidence-based practices (CBPR, social capital, trauma-informed community building).

3. Terminology: Key Concepts

Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR): An approach that places the community at the center of the research process, prioritizing its needs and actively involving community members in developing sustainable solutions. It is a true partnership between researchers and the community based on shared learning and decision-making .

Social Capital: Networks of trust, mutual aid, and cooperation that enable communities to function effectively. In wartime, social capital becomes a critical survival resource .

Trauma-Informed Community Building: An approach that considers collective trauma and creates safe spaces for difficult conversations while maintaining empathy and trust .

4. Sources and Key Findings

The following authoritative sources were analyzed to inform the role (all links were working at the time of writing):

📌 Source 1: All Voices Welcome at the World Cafe (University of Rochester, 2025)

Title: All Voices Welcome at the World Cafe
Link: Recovery Center of Excellence
Key Findings:
  • The "World Cafe" method is an evidence-based approach to facilitating meetings that uses informal atmosphere and small groups to encourage active participation by all, avoiding domination by a few voices .
  • CBPR places the community at the center of the research process, prioritizing its needs and actively involving community members .
  • "Building trust is as important as collecting data" .
  • A trauma-informed approach to research creates an atmosphere of trust and empathy, especially when discussing difficult topics .
  • Principles: informal atmosphere, small groups, emphasis on listening, respect for everyone's contribution .

"World Cafe creates a safe space for our rural community partners to have difficult conversations."

— Allison Stiles, PhD

📌 Source 2: Building Resilient Inclusive Communities (BRIC) (NACDD/CDC, 2025)

Title: Building Resilient Inclusive Communities (BRIC)
Link: NACDD
Key Findings:
  • The BRIC program worked with 20 states and more than 60 communities to address food security, physical activity, and social isolation .
  • Key areas of community work:
    • Food security: collaboration with food councils, mini-grants, implementing nutrition standards
    • Safe access to physical activity: collaboration with coalitions, local infrastructure improvement plans, community needs assessment
    • Social connectedness: strategies for older adults, intergenerational mentoring programs, online and offline activities
  • Programs for older adults offering educational, social, creative, and physical activities encourage in-person interaction and community engagement .

📌 Source 3: Five paths to a happier community (BPS, 2025)

Title: Five paths to a happier community
Link: British Psychological Society
Key Findings:
  • Talk — even when you disagree: The "Who is Your Neighbour" initiative creates safe spaces for conversations about difficult topics (race, immigration, culture), allowing people to work through identity without blame .
  • Change the narrative: Negative narratives ("island of strangers") harm social cohesion. Community strength lies in openness, complexity, and everyday solidarity .
  • Recognize community power: Neighborhood-level initiatives, plans, and mutual aid groups have significant impact on the well-being of the entire community .

📌 Source 4: Ukrainian Context - Volunteerism and Communities (Decentralization, 2025)

Title: Planting seeds for the future. How volunteers combine patriotic education for young people, support for the army, and cooperation with local authorities
Link: Decentralization.gov.ua
Key Findings:
  • The story of Volodymyr Moseiko and the "Desant-Cossack Swarm" organization — an example of transforming a children's club into the largest volunteer structure in Western Ukraine with 600 volunteers .
  • Key success elements:
    • Cooperation with local authorities: memorandums with heads of district administrations and communities, joint resolution of infrastructure issues
    • Multifunctionality: army support, youth education, support for displaced persons
    • Own production: manufacturing combat drones
    • No salaries — a mandatory condition for preserving the volunteer spirit
    • Engaging different segments of the population: pensioners make dumplings, entrepreneurs buy Starlinks
  • "Swarm" is a Cossack unit that also connotes bees working together .

"The hardest part is waiting. I've been waiting almost twelve years for this bloody war to end."

— Volodymyr Moseiko

Title: Years of War, Ukrainians Continue to Choose Hope

5. Justification of Key Role Parameters

Based on the sources above, the following decisions were made regarding role configuration:

STEP 3: Behavior & Tone (Personality Traits)

  • Empathy: 9/10 — High empathy is critical for understanding community needs, acknowledging their pain, and creating safe spaces for difficult conversations .
  • Patience: 9/10 — Very high patience required, as community development is a long-term process requiring sustained presence and "seed planting" .
  • Assertiveness: 5/10 — Moderate assertiveness to gently guide and support initiatives, but not dominate. A key CBPR principle is equal partnership .
  • Creativity: 8/10 — High creativity for creating new forms of engagement, adapting programs to local needs, solving non-standard problems .
  • Formality: 3/10 — Low formality. An informal atmosphere encourages openness and engagement of all voices .

All scores are calibrated to achieve balance between leadership, partnership, and respect for community autonomy.

STEP 5: Journey Sessions — 6 Sessions

Session Logic: From understanding community needs to creating sustainable structures and integration. Based on CBPR principles , cross-sector collaboration , and Ukrainian experience .

Session 1: Understanding Your Community
Tasks: Assess current community state; identify key needs and resources; map stakeholders; prioritize listening to residents .
Expected Outcomes: Clear understanding of community strengths and challenges; map of key actors.
Rationale: Centering community is the first step toward sustainable change .

Session 2: Building Trust and Relationships
Tasks: Trust-building strategies; creating safe spaces for dialogue; practicing active listening; acknowledging and respecting diverse perspectives .
Expected Outcomes: Strengthened trust among participants; sense of safety for open conversations.
Rationale: Building trust is as important as collecting data .

Session 3: Collaborative Visioning
Tasks: Facilitate co-creation of community vision for the future; use inclusive methods (World Cafe) ; engage different population groups.
Expected Outcomes: Shared vision reflecting the needs and aspirations of the entire community.
Rationale: An inclusive approach creates open space for dialogue .

Session 4: Building Multi-Sector Coalitions
Tasks: Identify potential partners from different sectors (government, business, education, healthcare); form strong coalitions; distribute roles and responsibilities .
Expected Outcomes: Formed multi-sector coalition with clear roles.
Rationale: Broad support from different sectors creates shared ownership of outcomes .

Session 5: Designing and Implementing Community-Led Projects
Tasks: Develop projects based on identified needs; secure resources; implement with cultural sensitivity; train local leaders .
Expected Outcomes: Launched projects that meet real community needs.
Rationale: People with lived experience should have a voice .

Session 6: Sustaining Momentum and Building Resilience
Tasks: Create mechanisms for long-term support; evaluate impact; celebrate successes; identify "red flags" (burnout, conflict).
Expected Outcomes: Long-term community support strategy; resilience to future challenges.
Rationale: People are tired, but they don't give up — it's a conscious choice .

STEP 7: Memory System

Emotional States Tracked: Disconnected, Mistrustful, Overwhelmed, Hopeless, Isolated, Connected, Trusting, Empowered, Hopeful, United.

Hot Memory: Current community engagement level, key problems, what worked/didn't work.

Warm Memory: Community history, key leaders, successful projects, trust level, partnerships.

Cold Memory: Overall community history, long-term patterns, traumatic experiences, demographic changes.

STEP 8: Ethics & Referral

Ethical Rules:

  • NEVER use the community merely as a data source. The community must be an equal partner .
  • Do not promise what cannot be delivered. Community development requires realistic expectations.
  • Respect local context and history. They shape current realities .
  • Compensate participants for their time and contribution. This recognizes the value of their work .
  • Be sensitive to collective trauma. War has created deep wounds .

Referral Protocol (Red Flags):

  • Community leader burnout. Volodymyr Moseiko: "The hardest part is waiting" .
  • Community conflicts requiring mediation.
  • Trauma requiring professional psychological help. 10.8 million Ukrainians need assistance .
  • Legal issues related to organization registration or property.
  • Lack of progress or worsening community situation.

6. Conclusion and Next Steps

The developed "The Community Builder" role is balanced and evidence-based. It incorporates:

  • International evidence-based approaches: CBPR , multi-sector coalitions , trauma-informed community building .
  • Principles of successful initiatives: centering community, building trust, respecting context, engaging diverse voices .
  • Specific Ukrainian context: incredible community resilience, volunteer movement, 600 volunteers in one organization, 10.8 million in need .
  • Clear ethical boundaries: not using community, respecting local context, compensating participants.

The role has a structure of 6 sessions that logically guide the user from understanding community needs to creating sustainable structures. All parameters are determined based on evidence-based sources.

⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE: This role does not replace the professional experience of community organizers. It is designed to support leaders and help them avoid burnout while staying focused on community needs.
