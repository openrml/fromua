Restoring Connection with the Body After Trauma: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Body After Trauma Guide" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainians who have experienced war trauma — veterans, civilians in combat zones, people who have lost loved ones, forcibly displaced persons.

Trauma is not just a psychological but also a deeply bodily experience. Bessel van der Kolk in his classic work "The Body Keeps the Score" emphasizes: "Dissociation is the essence of trauma. The overwhelming experience is fragmented, so that emotions, sounds, images, thoughts, and physical sensations associated with the trauma begin to live their own lives" [1].

Key Findings

🔹 Trauma Disrupts Sensory Processing
Trauma affects how the brain processes sensory information. The vestibular system (responsible for balance and spatial orientation) and somatosensory system (responsible for touch, pressure, temperature) can be disrupted in people with PTSD. This causes either hypersensitivity or, conversely, numbness and feelings of detachment from the body [2].
🔹 Dissociation as Disconnection
Clients with dissociative symptoms may feel numb or disconnected from their sensations [3]. Restoring connection takes time and a safe pace.
🔹 Body-Based Therapies
Somatic, body-oriented psychotherapies (such as Somatic Experiencing, Sensorimotor Psychotherapy) focus on interoceptive awareness and regulation of nervous system arousal through attention to bodily states [4].
🔹 The Importance of Safety
It's important to engage clients in bodily awareness at a pace they can tolerate. For this, the capacity for dual awareness is strengthened: simultaneous awareness of external sensations and internal bodily signals [5].
🔹 Practical Techniques
Somatic exercises help release trauma, reduce anxiety and chronic pain [6]. These include: body scanning, grounding, breathing techniques, mindful movement (yoga, tai chi).
🔹 Integration of Past and Present
Processes like the Trauma Reintegration Process (TRP) help integrate dissociated aspects of experience, allowing the client to feel wholeness, "to look through the eyes of the present" [7].

Practical Techniques for Restoring Body Connection

🧘 Dual Awareness
Simultaneous attention to the body AND to the safe space around. For example: "I feel tension in my shoulders AND at the same time I hear the sound of rain outside." This prevents re-traumatization.
🪑 Grounding Through Supports
Sit on a chair, feel the points of contact: feet to floor, sitting bones to chair, back to backrest. Explore pressure, weight, temperature. If there are no sensations — simply note "it's quiet here for now."
🔍 Body Scan (Safe)
Slowly move attention through your body, from toes to crown. Don't look for pain or tension — just observe. If an area is numb, note "it's quiet here for now." You can always stop.
🌬️ Breath as a Bridge to the Body
Place a hand on your belly or chest, feel the movement. Observe how your hand rises and falls. If there are no sensations — just breathe and observe your hand.
✋ Temperature and Texture
Touch different surfaces: a cold window, a warm cup, a soft blanket, a smooth stone. Focus on sensations with your fingertips.
🧱 Body Boundaries
Slowly run your palms over your body — from shoulders to fingertips, from thighs to feet. Feel where your body "ends" and space begins.
🚶 Mindful Movement
Stretch very slowly, roll your shoulders a few times, sway from side to side. Observe sensations during movement, don't try to do it "right."
💎 Resource Place in the Body
Find an area with no pain, tension, or numbness (e.g., a pinky finger, palm, knee). Focus on it, explore the sensations. This is a "safe base" to return to.

Ukrainian Context

Resources for Working with Body Trauma in Ukraine:

  • Psychotherapists specializing in trauma — somatic therapists, EMDR/SE practitioners
  • Rehabilitation centers for veterans — with body-oriented programs
  • Lifeline Ukraine — crisis hotline: 7333
  • Support groups — for people who have experienced trauma
  • Yoga studios with trauma-informed teachers — gentle practices

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Thoughts of self-harm ("I want to hurt my body")
  • Complete lack of body sensation for weeks
  • Panic attacks when trying to feel the body
  • Dissociation where you don't remember what you've been doing for extended periods
  • The feeling that your body is "not yours"
