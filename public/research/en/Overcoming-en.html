Overcoming Isolation and Building Social Connections: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Community Connection Builder" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainians experiencing isolation due to war — forced displacement, loss of familiar social circles, language barriers, social anxiety after traumatic events.

Loneliness is not just the absence of people around, but the subjective feeling of a gap between desired and actual social relationships [1]. Research shows that social isolation and loneliness increase the risk of premature death by 29-32% [2]. At the same time, quality friendships protect against depression and anxiety, reduce the brain's stress response, and even improve physical health [3].

Key Strategies for Overcoming Isolation

🔹 Small Steps Instead of Global Changes
If social anxiety is high, start small: short walks in familiar places, visits with a friend or family member, clear awareness of exits to be able to leave quickly if panic arises [4]. Gradually increasing time spent in social situations helps adaptation.
🔹 Volunteering as a Path to Community
Volunteering not only increases opportunities for social interactions but also positively impacts sense of identity and belonging [5]. It's a way to become an active participant, not a passive recipient of help.
🔹 Interest-Based Groups (Meet-up Groups)
Many cities have groups organized around shared interests or hobbies: hiking, gaming, book clubs, conversation clubs [6]. Platforms like Meetup.com help find like-minded people.
🔹 Social Prescribing
This strategy involves healthcare professionals referring patients to non-medical community activities: creative workshops, walking groups, social clubs [7].
🔹 Initiative in Relationships
Research shows that people tend to overestimate the awkwardness of reconnecting with old contacts and underestimate the positive feelings it evokes [8]. Sending a simple "I was thinking of you" message can be a start.
🔹 Microconnections
Quick chats with neighbors, greeting a barista, a short call to a friend — these daily "microconnections" are also important for improving mood and sense of belonging [9].
🔹 Virtual Communities as a Bridge
Online groups can be a safe way to practice social skills and reduce anxiety before in-person meetings [10]. For people with limited mobility or in emergency situations, virtual social events may be the only way to maintain connections.
🔹 Overcoming Negative Thinking
Loneliness is often accompanied by negative thoughts that can become obstacles to positive change. It's important to learn to notice these thoughts and gently shift them [11].
🔹 Shared Activity as the Foundation of Friendship
Research shows that moving from acquaintance to friendship takes approximately 50 hours of shared time [12]. It's important to create opportunities for regular meetings.

Ukrainian Context: Where to Find Communities

Platforms for Finding Communities in Ukraine:

  • Meetup.com — international platform for interest-based groups
  • Facebook Groups — by city, interests, professions
  • Telegram Channels — local communities, volunteer groups
  • "SpivDiya" Platform — volunteer initiatives
  • Veteran Spaces — for veterans and their families
  • Language Clubs — for learning languages and meeting people

Volunteer Organizations:

  • Red Cross of Ukraine
  • Caritas Ukraine
  • "Come Back Alive" Foundation
  • Local animal shelters

Useful Resources:

  • Lifeline Ukraine — crisis hotline: 7333
  • Psychological support — for working with social anxiety

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Inability to leave the house for months
  • Panic attacks when trying to talk to people
  • Thoughts that "no one needs me"
  • Suicidal thoughts due to loneliness
  • Inability to get out of bed
