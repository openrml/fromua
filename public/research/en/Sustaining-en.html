Sustaining Long-Distance Relationships: Evidence Base

This overview serves as the evidence base for the "Long-Distance Relationship Sustainer" role on fromua.life. The information is adapted for Ukrainian couples forcibly separated by war — one partner at the front, the other abroad or in a safer region.

Long-distance relationships are not just a challenge, but also an opportunity for deeper emotional connection. Research shows that couples in such relationships can be as satisfied as geographically close couples, and sometimes even more so [1]. This is because partners often approach time together more consciously and regulate their behavior better [2].

Key Findings

🔹 Shared Vision of the Future
Long-distance relationships need a shared long-term vision. It's important to discuss whether you plan to live together in the future, when this might happen, and what each of you will do to achieve this goal [3]. This creates a sense of progress and shared purpose. Circumstances can change, so it's important to return to these questions regularly.
🔹 Three Phases of Relationships
Long-distance relationships are a cycle consisting of three phases: time together (physical meetings), time before separation, and time apart [4]. Each phase requires different strategies for maintaining connection.
🟢 TOGETHER
Physical meetings
🟡 BEFORE SEPARATION
Preparing for goodbye
🔴 APART
Maintaining connection
🔹 Balance During Meetings
When you're together, it's important to find balance between intensity and relaxation. Don't try to fill every minute with activities. Time spent quietly together (watching a movie, reading books) is equally important for nervous system regulation and feeling close [5].
🔹 Separation is Also an Art
Before parting, it's important to allow time for sadness, confirm plans for the next meeting, and not isolate after the partner leaves.
🔹 Maintaining Connection at a Distance
Research identifies various ways to maintain intimacy: from digital emotions (video calls, shared online activities) to online sexual activity and finding meaning through spirituality [6]. The key is regular, mindful communication and shared rituals.
🔹 Self-Care and Individuality
It's important to maintain your own emotional health, pursue your interests, and allow your partner to do the same. Healthy relationships consist of two whole individuals [7].

Practical Strategies for Each Phase

🟢 "Together" Phase

  • Plan both active adventures and time "just being together" — sitting in silence, reading books, watching movies is also quality time.
  • Don't create pressure for "perfect weekends" — allow yourselves to just be together.
  • Discuss after meetings — what was best, what was missing.

🟡 "Before Separation" Phase

  • Acknowledge sadness — don't avoid these feelings, experience them together.
  • Confirm next meeting plans before leaving — this creates hope.
  • Create a goodbye ritual — something that will be just yours.
  • Don't isolate after departure — reach out to friends, family.

🔴 "Apart" Phase

  • Connection rituals: watching movies together, reading the same book, online games, cooking the same meal at a distance.
  • Communication: regular check-ins, "deep" conversations instead of superficial ones, scheduling video dates.
  • Working with jealousy: explore triggers, use self-soothing, talk about jealousy without blame.
  • Sexual intimacy: discuss comfortable formats, remember consent and safety.
  • Individual life: develop hobbies, sports, learning, maintain friendships — this makes you more interesting to each other.

Ukrainian Context

For Ukrainian couples separated by war, important resources include:

  • Psychological support for military and their families — Veteran Hub, "Come Back Alive" Foundation
  • Support lines — Lifeline Ukraine (crisis support)
  • Support groups for military spouses — on social media and in veteran spaces
  • Communication opportunities — stable internet, video calling apps

When Professional Help is Needed

Red Flags:
  • Violence in the relationship (physical, psychological)
  • Threats of self-harm from partner
  • Suicidal thoughts due to separation
  • Partner has disappeared and been out of contact for a long time
  • Serious conflicts that cannot be resolved independently
