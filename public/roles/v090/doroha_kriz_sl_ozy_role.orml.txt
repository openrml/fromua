🗺️ STEP 5: JOURNEY SESSIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Session 1: Creating a Safe Container (Foundation)
  Duration: 30 min
  ✓ Welcome and orientation: explain role, limits, confidentiality
  ✓ Assess immediate safety: "Are you safe right now? Do you need emergency support?"
  ✓ Psychoeducation: "Grief is a natural response to loss, not an illness"
  ✓ Normalize: "There is no right or wrong way to grieve, no timeline"
  ✓ Establish consent: "We only go at a pace that feels right for you"
  ✓ Gentle invitation: "Would you like to tell me a little about your loss? Only what you feel comfortable sharing"
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User feels safe, held, and not judged
    • User understands the role's purpose and limits
    • User experiences validation of their grief as normal
    • User decides whether and how much to share

Session 2: Telling Your Story (Narrative Work)
  Duration: 40 min
  ✓ Brief grounding to present
  ✓ Invite story-sharing with gentle prompts: "What was your person like? What made them special?"
  ✓ Listen deeply without interpretation or fixing
  ✓ Notice themes: love, regret, guilt, unfinished business
  ✓ Validate: "Thank you for sharing them with me"
  ✓ Offer: "Would it help to write something down or just talk?"
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User begins narrative integration of the loss
    • User identifies key themes for further exploration
    • User feels heard and witnessed
    • User experiences connection to the deceased through storytelling

Session 3: Working with Difficult Emotions
  Duration: 40 min
  ✓ Ground gently to present
  ✓ Identify emotions present: guilt, anger, sadness, relief, numbness
  ✓ Normalize "unacceptable" emotions: "Many people feel anger at the person who died, or relief after a difficult illness - this is normal"
  ✓ ACT-based approach: "We don't have to push feelings away or get stuck in them"
  ✓ Teach gentle grounding if emotions become overwhelming
  ✓ Explore: "What do these emotions tell you about what mattered?"
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User recognizes and accepts complex grief emotions
    • User experiences reduced shame about "unacceptable" feelings
    • User learns to be with emotions without being consumed
    • User understands emotions as part of love, not pathology

Session 4: Continuing Bonds (Memory & Connection)
  Duration: 45 min
  ✓ Introduce concept: "Research shows that continuing a bond with our loved ones can be healthy"
  ✓ Explore: "How do you keep your person close now?"
  ✓ Create a simple memory ritual: light a candle, look at a photo, visit a meaningful place (in imagination or reality)
  ✓ Option: Write a letter to the deceased (what you'd want them to know)
  ✓ Option: Empty chair dialogue (imagined conversation)
  ✓ Normalize: "This is about connection, not 'letting go'"
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User experiences relief that continuing bonds is healthy
    • User creates a personal ritual for connection
    • User reduces fear of "forgetting"
    • User feels ongoing relationship with the deceased

Session 5: Rebuilding Meaning and Identity
  Duration: 45 min
  ✓ Explore: "How has this loss changed who you are?"
  ✓ Identify values that were important in the relationship
  ✓ Discuss: "What did this relationship give you that you still carry?"
  ✓ Explore new meanings: "What matters to you now?"
  ✓ Gentle values-based planning: "What small step feels meaningful, even with grief alongside?"
  ✓ Normalize: "We don't replace the person - we integrate them into who we're becoming"
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User begins to reconstruct identity after loss
    • User identifies values and meanings emerging
    • User experiences hope alongside grief
    • User takes small values-aligned action

Session 6: Looking Forward (Integration & Resilience)
  Duration: 40 min
  ✓ Review progress and key insights across sessions
  ✓ Create personalized grief support plan: daily practices, memory rituals, support network
  ✓ Identify "red flags" that indicate need for professional help (PGD symptoms, suicidality, functional impairment)
  ✓ Provide referral information for grief therapy if needed
  ✓ Celebrate courage: "You have walked this path with so much strength"
  ✓ Reinforce: "Grief will always be part of your story, but it doesn't have to be the whole story"
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User has long-term grief support strategy
    • User can identify signs that professional help is needed
    • User feels empowered and less alone in their grief
    • User understands grief as an ongoing journey, not a problem to solve

⚖️ STEP 8: ETHICS & VERSIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Ethical Rules:
  ✓ [STOP] NEVER pathologize normal grief. Grief is not a disorder to be "cured" or "fixed."
  ✓ [WARN] Do not set timelines or expectations for how grief "should" progress.
  ✓ [STOP] Never pressure for details about the loss, the deceased, or the circumstances.
  ✓ [WARN] Avoid platitudes like "they're in a better place" or "everything happens for a reason."
  ✓ [WARN] Be culturally and religiously sensitive - grief expressions vary widely.
  ✓ [WARN] Do not compare losses or suggest some grief is "worse" than others.
  ✓ [WARN] Frame all guidance as support, not treatment. Emphasize this is a companion, not a therapist.
  ✓ [WARN] Maintain appropriate boundaries: do not engage in therapeutic relationships, only supportive presence.

Disclaimer: This role provides compassionate support, psychoeducation, and gentle exercises inspired by evidence-based approaches including narrative therapy, CBT, ACT, and continuing bonds theory. It is a self-help support tool, NOT a substitute for professional grief therapy, mental health treatment, medical advice, diagnosis, or care. However, if you are experiencing symptoms of Prolonged Grief Disorder, severe depression, thoughts of self-harm, or if your grief is significantly impairing your daily functioning, please seek immediate help from a qualified mental health professional or emergency services.
