🗺️ STEP 5: JOURNEY SESSIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Session 1: Being Here in the Dark — It's Okay to Have No Reason (Arriving)
  Duration: 35 min
  ✓ Welcome and orientation: explain role, limits, confidentiality
  ✓ ASSESS SAFETY IMMEDIATELY: "Are you safe right now? Do you have thoughts of harming yourself?"
  ✓ If safety concern → initiate referral protocol immediately
  ✓ Create safe, non-judgmental space
  ✓ Normalize: "It's okay to have no reason to wake up. It's okay to be lost."
  ✓ Validate: "You've carried something heavy here. That already matters."
  ✓ No pressure to "find meaning" — just being together is enough
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User feels safe and accepted in their darkness
    • User understands this is not a place for answers, but for companionship
    • Safety has been assessed and addressed
    • Trust begins to form

Session 2: Recognizing the Questions — What's the Pain Asking? (Listening)
  Duration: 40 min
  ✓ Brief check-in and safety reassessment
  ✓ Gentle exploration: "What questions have been visiting you lately?"
  ✓ Acknowledge the source of these questions — loss, trauma, exhaustion, war
  ✓ Introduce Frankl's "tragic triad" gently: suffering, guilt, death — these are the places where meaning is questioned
  ✓ Explore: "Before all this, what gave you a sense of meaning, even a little?"
  ✓ Identify any "sparks" — fragments of what once mattered
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User begins to articulate existential questions
    • User connects questions to real experiences
    • User identifies faint traces of past meaning
    • User feels less alone with their questions

Session 3: Discovering Sparks — Micro-Moments of Meaning (Noticing)
  Duration: 40 min
  ✓ Brief check-in and safety reassessment
  ✓ Introduce concept: "Meaning doesn't have to be grand. Sometimes it lives in tiny sparks."
  ✓ Explore micro-moments: warm tea, sunlight, a memory, a kind word, a bird outside
  ✓ Use dereflection gently: "What if, just for a moment, we shift from 'why?' to 'what do I notice?'"
  ✓ Example from practice: a displaced woman who found meaning in arranging her new home
  ✓ Identify 1-2 micro-sparks the user can notice this week
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User experiences that meaning can exist in small moments
    • User identifies at least one micro-spark
    • User feels a tiny shift in perspective
    • User has a gentle practice for the week

Session 4: The Attitude We Choose — Meaning in Suffering (Choosing)
  Duration: 40 min
  ✓ Brief check-in and safety reassessment
  ✓ Introduce Frankl's key insight: "When we cannot change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves."
  ✓ Explore: "Even in this darkness, is there any tiny space where you have a choice about how you meet it?"
  ✓ Share Captain Pylypey's story: choosing to create a "battlefield" in captivity, teaching English, saying "one day closer to home"
  ✓ Discuss: "What would it mean to choose even one small attitude toward what you're carrying?"
  ✓ No pressure — just exploration
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User understands that attitude can be a source of meaning
    • User sees an example of extraordinary resilience
    • User explores their own potential for choice, however tiny
    • User feels a flicker of agency

Session 5: Rewriting the Story — From "Why Me?" to "What Now?" (Transforming)
  Duration: 45 min
  ✓ Brief check-in and safety reassessment
  ✓ Explore the current life narrative: "What story are you telling yourself about all this?"
  ✓ Gently introduce possibility of reframing: "What if the question shifted from 'why me?' to 'what now?'"
  ✓ Use Socratic dialogue to explore new perspectives
  ✓ Look for "new possibilities" that might have emerged through loss (PTG)
  ✓ Example: a mother who created a memory ritual for her lost sons
  ✓ Begin to weave a new narrative thread — one that includes both pain and survival
  Expected Outcomes:
    • User begins to see their story as something they can participate in shaping
    • User identifies a new narrative possibility
    • User experiences a sense of authorship, however small
    • User feels a tiny opening
